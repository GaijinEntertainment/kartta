# Resolve a build's extractions 1,024 files at a time

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Updated:** 2026-10-21
- **Initiative:** `03-code-graph`
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta/src/resolving.rs` (`RESOLVED_AT_ONCE`, `Resolving::insert_all`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta/tests/engine.rs` (`a_tree_of_more_files_than_one_batch_resolves_whole`)

A build resolves its extractions a batch at a time once the walk is done
([ADR 3.6](/design-docs/adr/3.6-graph-build-pass.md)). A batch's decoded extractions are held beside the graphs
grown so far and the encodings not yet resolved, so the batch sets how far the build peaks above what it keeps. A batch
is 1,024 files, which trades a lower peak for resolution a fraction of a second longer.

## Decision

- A build resolves 1,024 files at a time, in path order, and inserts each batch before it resolves the next.
- A refresh that resolves files again batches them the same way.

## Why

- Once the walk's pages are trimmed, glibc's resident set peaks in resolution, at the batch: on Linux in a container of
  4 CPUs, cold, the C++ game-engine tree peaked at 1,022 MiB with 1,024 files a batch against 1,079 MiB with 4,096
  (2026-10-21).
- Below 1,024 the peak barely moves and resolution slows: 512 files took resolution up to 1.9 times as long on macOS.

## Consequences

- Resolution takes up to 0.4 s longer than at 4,096 files on the game-engine tree on macOS with 18 threads, and is
  faster under musl; no Linux build moved beyond its spread; accepted.
- Where the walk sets the peak, as on the Go monorepo's cold builds on macOS, the batch moves nothing; accepted.

## Rejected

- **4,096 files at a time** — 57 MiB more of glibc's resident peak on the game-engine tree, for 10 MiB less resident
  after a musl build of it.
- **512 at a time** — at most 9 MiB less heap than 1,024, for resolution up to 1.9 times as long on macOS.
- **2,048 at a time** — up to 23 MiB above 1,024's peaks on the Go monorepo and under glibc, and no faster beyond the
  spread.
- **16,384 at a time** — 201 MiB more of glibc's peak than 4,096 on the game-engine tree.
- **A batch bounded by the bytes of its encodings rather than by files** — sized to what it decodes, which a tree of
  uneven files would show; not built, since at 1,024 files a batch's time showed only with 18 threads on macOS.

## Revisit if

The call graph or a tree of uneven files moves the build's peak so that a batch of 1,024 files no longer sets it, or
sets it by its largest files. Medium confidence: measured on two trees.
