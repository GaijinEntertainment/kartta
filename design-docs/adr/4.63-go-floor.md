# Name the Go module's floor in its `go` directive, and run CI at it

- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Governs:** `bindings/go/go.mod`, the `go` job of `.github/workflows/ci.yml`
- **Pinned by:** the floor entry of the `go` job of `.github/workflows/ci.yml`

A library's `go` directive is a demand on every consumer, and the Go binding of
[ADR 4.2](/design-docs/adr/4.2-c-abi-wire.md) is a library. Its directive names the oldest toolchain the module runs
on, and CI runs the binding there, at the cost of a floor kept in two places.

## Decision

- **The Go floor.** The module's `go` directive, `go 1.25.0`, names the oldest toolchain the module runs on, and pins
  nothing of the repository's own build. CI runs the binding on a pinned newer toolchain on each platform and at the
  floor on Linux, which fails on a directive raised past it and on a symbol or a behavior newer than it.

## Why

- A library's `go` directive is a demand on its consumers: each must run that toolchain or a later one. The suite passes
  at `go 1.25.0` on go1.25.0 and fails at `go 1.24.0` on `sync.WaitGroup.Go` alone (2026-11-02).
- A newer toolchain cannot stand in for the floor. Its `stdversion` check sees a newer symbol, but no raised directive
  and no changed behavior: `encoding/json` words the error for a bad escape differently on go1.25.0 and go1.27.2.

## Consequences

- The floor repeats in `go.mod` and in the `go` job's matrix, and raising it means changing both. Accepted: the job
  fails until both move, so no edit to `go.mod` alone raises the demand on consumers.
- The floor entry runs no `govulncheck`, which needs Go 1.26 to run; it scans the pinned toolchain alone. A consumer who
  builds on an old patch release takes that release's fixes on themselves. Accepted.

## Rejected

- **`go 1.27.2`** — every consumer would need that toolchain for nothing the module uses past 1.25 (#1052).
- **`go 1.24.0`** — the library builds there, but it takes a test rewritten around `sync.WaitGroup.Go` to reach a
  toolchain that cannot read the `go-import` subdirectory #131 decides on (#1052).

## Revisit if

The Go floor follows what the module uses, so a change that needs a newer toolchain, or a consumer held below the
floor, reopens it.

## History

- 2026-11-02 — The Go floor: `go 1.27.2` pinned the build and demanded that toolchain of every consumer, so the
  directive names the floor and CI runs at it (#1052)
