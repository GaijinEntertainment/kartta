# List a wide node's children through a cursor and a short node's by index

- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-lang/src/tree.rs` (`child_nodes`, `named_child_nodes`),
  `crates/kartta-lang-go/src/walk/uses.rs` (`field_places`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-lang-jsts/src/tests/extraction.rs`
  (`eight_times_the_top_level_comments_cost_about_eight_times_the_walk`,
  `the_child_helpers_list_what_indexing_lists_at_every_width`), `crates/kartta-lang-go/tests/extract.rs`
  (`eight_times_the_top_level_comments_cost_about_eight_times_the_walk`,
  `eight_times_the_comments_in_one_field_cost_about_eight_times_a_positional_literal`)

Indexing a tree-sitter node's children restarts from its first child on each index, which is quadratic over a flat
run of children, and comments are spliced flat into their parent. A cursor steps in linear time and allocates. The
helpers index a node of up to 16 children and list a wider one through a cursor, which keeps the cost of a short node
at the price of two paths.

## Decision

- **Short nodes index.** `child_nodes` and `named_child_nodes` index a node of at most 16 children of the kind they
  list. This allocates nothing, and a caller that stops early stops the work.
- **Wide nodes list.** Past 16 they collect the children through a cursor before the caller takes the first one, so
  every call on a wide node costs its whole width, and iteration runs both ways from the list.
- **A hand-written walk over a node's children steps a cursor.** It does not call `field_name_for_child` or `child` in a
  loop over indexes.

## Why

tree-sitter's `ts_node_child` starts at the first child on every call (`src/node.c`, `ts_node__child`, tree-sitter
0.27.1). A hidden repeat is a balanced tree it can skip, so a list of statements stays cheap, but extras sit flat: one
TypeScript file of 583,711 comment lines took one worker 1,278 s, and a build's cancel waited for it.

A cursor for every call cost 1.81% more instructions on the Go monorepo snapshot's extraction, 306.4 G to 311.9 G, and
this rule costs 0.02%, 306.31 G to 306.37 G, and 0.14% on dagor, 1,581.6 G to 1,583.8 G, 2026-11-02: most calls see
few children and many stop at the first match. One call on three children costs 33 ns indexed and 97 ns listed, and the
two meet between 17 and 25 children.

## Consequences

- A caller that asks for the first child of the same wide node many times pays the width each time, 48 µs at 1,025
  children against 7 ns: accepted, since no walk does so outside a loop already linear in that width.
- A list holds 32 bytes per child while its caller iterates, freed when the iterator drops: accepted.
- `prev_sibling`, `next_sibling` and `parent` still restart from the parent or the root, a cost this rule does not
  reach: a file nested 16,000 deep walks in 7 s to 28 s by language, and that time grows with the square of the depth
  (#1134).

## Rejected

- A cursor and a list on every call: 1.81% more instructions on the snapshot.
- A lazy cursor that lists only for reverse iteration: a custom iterator type, and a full walk of three children costs
  90 ns against 33 ns indexed, so it stays near the list's cost on real trees.
- An index limit of 32: 0.17% on the snapshot against 0.13%, no gain.

## Revisit if

- tree-sitter's `ts_node_child` stops restarting, which removes the wide path; high confidence that it holds today,
  since the cost comes from its child iterator.
- `Node` gains a cursor that needs no allocation, which removes the short path.
