# Break a Quirrel member call's tie by the tables its receiver reaches

- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-graph/src/scope.rs` (`scoped`, `one_owner_reached_by_name`, `spells`),
  `crates/kartta-lang-quirrel/src/lib.rs` (`CONVENTIONS`), `kartta_lang::UntypedOwners`
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-languages/tests/quirrel_tables.rs`,
  `crates/kartta-graph/tests/graph/calls.rs::a_member_call_binds_past_a_namesake_of_a_table_it_does_not_reach`

A Quirrel module table owns the functions it declares
([ADR 3.143](/design-docs/adr/3.143-quirrel-function-names.md)), and a table is one value that a caller names.
Where a member call on another value meets the method of a class and the namesake of a table in one scope, the table's
member drops out and the class's binds; a table's member that no other owner ties still binds, whatever the receiver.

## Decision

- **Tables.** Where `Conventions::untyped_owners` is `UntypedOwners::Tables`, as it is for Quirrel, an owner that no
  type of its name declares is a table; every other language keeps `UntypedOwners::Types`. A member call reaches a table
  through a receiver whose last segment spells it in any case, `psn.sessionManager.update()` for `sessionManager`, or
  from a caller that is a member of it, `this.f()` inside one of its functions.
- **Ties.** Where the definitions one scope tier offers a member call, the caller's file, its package or its includes,
  sit on several owners, the members of tables the call does not reach drop out. One owner left binds; none left or
  several leave the tie, and the call goes to the tiers below.
- **Other tiers.** No other tier reads the rule: a member call that one table's member alone answers binds it.

## Why

- A table is reached by its name or from its own members, while a class's method is called on any value of the class:
  `feed.getMainRequest(event)` holds the `Feed` instance a module returns, not the `_scores` table beside
  it. On the game-engine tree the rule binds 21 member calls the tie left unbound, 21 of 21 right (2026-10-23, #392).
- An importer may bind a table under another name, `let sheets = require(...)`, or slot functions into it from another
  file that call it through `this`, so dropping a table's member where nothing ties it loses right pairs.

## Consequences

- A call whose receiver renames a table still ties where a class's namesake shares the scope. Accepted.
- A receiver that spells a table does not drop a class's namesake, so `x.t.f()` beside a class's `f` still ties.
  Accepted.

## Rejected

- **Drop an unreached table's member from every tier** — lost 133 pairs on that tree, about 60 of them right: tables
  renamed by their importer or extended from another file; of the 34 pairs it added, 4 were wrong.
- **Type the value a module returns under the name it is imported by** — needs the walk to record what a file-scope
  binding holds and what the module's returned table names, an extraction the graph does not have.

## Revisit if

The Quirrel walk records the type a file-scope binding holds. Held with high confidence for the tie itself.
