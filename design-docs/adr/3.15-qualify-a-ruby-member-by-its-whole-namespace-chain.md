# Qualify a Ruby member by its whole namespace chain

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Revised:** 2026-10-23
- **Initiative:** `03-code-graph`

Ruby defines much of its structure through method calls that run in a class body: `include` and `extend` add parents,
`attr_accessor` defines methods, `private` changes what is exported, and `alias_method` gives a method a second name.
Ruby also reopens a type in any file, and two libraries often hold types of one name in different namespaces,
`A::Worker` and `B::Worker`. The extraction reads one file and runs no code.

**A member is qualified by the whole chain of its type.** The owner of a member is the `::`-joined chain from the
outermost enclosing type, `Geo::Shape`, and the member's name extends it: `Geo::Shape#area` for an instance method,
`Geo::Shape.build` for a class method, `Geo::Shape::MAX` for a constant or a nested type. Two types of one name keep
their members apart, and a call on `self` names the chain of the caller's own type. A `class << self` block is
transparent, and its `def` is a class method. A method ends the chain, so a type that a method holds belongs to no
outer type. `def Foo.x` belongs to the nearest enclosing type named `Foo`, else to `Foo` as written.

**A class-body macro is structure only with no receiver.** `include`, `extend` and `prepend` add their constant
arguments to the parents of the enclosing type. `attr_reader :x` defines `Owner#x`, `attr_writer :x` defines
`Owner#x=`, and `attr_accessor` both. A bare `private`, `protected` or `public` sets whether the methods after it in
the body are exported, `private :x` changes a method the body has already declared, and `private def x` changes one
method. A `class << self` body keeps a visibility of its own, which covers its class methods, and
`private_class_method :x` changes a class method from the class body. A bare `private` in a class body leaves a
`def self.x` exported, as Ruby does. `module_function` is not tracked. A macro stays a recorded call beside the
structure it adds. The same words with a receiver, `Other.include Comparable`, are calls of methods of their own, and
add no structure.

**An alias is one callable with the body its original names at the alias.** When the file declares the original
before the alias, in the same type, kind and enclosing method, the two symbols are one callable: the original's body
defines it, and the alias declares it. A later redefinition of the original leaves the alias on the earlier body, as
Ruby does. A later declaration of the alias's own name replaces the alias, so that alias joins nothing.

**A require of a repository file is an include, and any other require an import.** A `require` or `require_relative`
with no receiver and a string argument of plain text names a file, with or without its `.rb`. A string with an
interpolation, `"cmd/#{name}"`, builds the path at run time, and one with an escape spells it apart from its value, so
either names nothing rather than an import of its spelling. `require_relative` resolves from the requiring file's
directory. `require` resolves under the first of `lib`, the root, `test`, `spec` and `app` that holds the file, the
load paths that Ruby projects set most often. A require that names no file of the repository, of a gem or of the
standard library, is an import as written. A Rails application loads most of its files by autoloading, without a
require, so many of its files have no include.

**A bare name records no call.** A call with no receiver, no arguments and no parentheses, `setup`, parses as a name,
the same as a read of a local variable. To tell the two apart, the extraction needs the bindings of each scope, and an
error there adds a wrong edge. `setup()` parses as a call and records one.

On Homebrew, 2,373 Ruby files give 22,480 symbols, 163,098 calls and 4,846 requires: 3,897 includes and 949 imports.
Six more requires interpolate their path. The visibility of a `class << self` body and `private_class_method` hide
161 class methods.

**Revised 2026-10-23: a call in a type's body is made by the type, on the type.** A call outside every method was a
call from file scope, so the class or module that made it had no callees, and under ADR 3.23's file-scope rule its name
reached every definition of it in the file: `track :size` in one class kept another class's `track` live, and
`find_callers` of either listed the file. A class or module body runs with `self` as the type, so the innermost type
whose body holds a call outside every method makes it, a block's in that body included, and a call there with no
receiver, or on `self`, records the type's chain as its receiver, the way `self.track` inside a method records the
owner: `track :size` binds `Widget.track`. A macro the walk reads as structure is such a call too. A use the body makes
through a symbol was already the type's.

- **Measured on Homebrew's library at `8f10429b56`,** 8,703 Ruby files: 5,386 extract differently and no file of
  another language does. 72,538 calls move from file scope to 9,668 classes and modules, 53,137 of them made by a
  class.
- **Edges:** 6,548 caller and callee pairs are added and none removed: 449 at 85, 675 at 80, 132 at 70, 2,512 at 60,
  1,006 at 50 and 1,774 at 30. By hand, seed 187, the tiers bind right at 9 of 10 at 85 (`register_scheme 'HTTPS'`,
  `status_code(38)` in each Bundler error), 7 of 10 at 80, 6 of 10 at 70, 9 of 10 at 60 (Sorbet's `sig { ... .void }`
  and `T.nilable`), 2 of 12 at 50 and 5 of 12 at 30 (Homebrew's `switch` and `conflicts` in a `cmd_args do` block): the
  3,768 pairs at 60 and above about 85% right, the 2,780 below about 33%. The wrong ones are the cascade's guesses for
  a runtime method on a literal or a constant, whose receiver the walk records empty, `[...].freeze` taking
  `Gem::Version#freeze` and `Hash.new` an `OptionParser#new`, and a mixin's or a require's name taking a namesake,
  `include` taking `Formula#include`; the same calls bind the same way from a method, where `main`'s pairs at 50 and 30
  sample at 2 of 10 each. Overall about 63% right, under the 90% a rule is taken at, every wrong one a guess of a tier
  that a method's call takes too.
- **A call with no receiver kept bare instead,** with the type as its caller: 3,777 pairs are added, about 66% right,
  but `track :size` binds no `Widget.track`, since the bare tiers look for the caller's owner's members and a type's
  owner is the type around it, and the 402 pairs at 85 that the type's receiver adds, `register_scheme` among them, are
  lost.
- **Dead code:** `dead_code` reports 27,223 Ruby methods where it reported 27,449, and 586 functions where it reported
  592, adding none. The uncertain methods grow by 478, the 226 that left the list, 193 that were live and 59 that were
  dispatched: a call that binds no definition takes the place of the file-scope name that reached every namesake.
- **Cost:** a call site takes the place of a file-scope name, so the library's Rust heap holds 1.01 MiB more after the
  build, 35.05 against 34.04 MiB, and peaks 1.0 MiB higher, by a counting allocator: about 15 bytes for each of the
  72,538 sites. At 2 threads under a load of 15 to 21, six alternating rounds took 4.8-5.6 s against 4.6-6.5 s cold
  and 0.50-1.25 s against 0.50-1.26 s warm, at a warm peak footprint of 101-103 MiB against 87-100 MiB.

## Considered Options

- **An owner by its last name, `Worker`** — one string less per member, but `A::Worker#run` and `B::Worker#run` become
  one name, and a call binds to either.
- **An import for every require, also one that resolves** — keeps the specifier as written beside the include, but
  states one dependency twice, and the contract keeps an import for a module that names no repository file.
- **A call for a bare name that the file does not bind** — finds `setup`, but needs every binding form of each scope,
  block parameters and patterns included, and a form it misses turns a variable read into a wrong edge.

Revisit when the extraction reads a `Gemfile` or the `$LOAD_PATH` additions of a project.
