# Credit a Ruby call in a type's body to the type

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Updated:** 2026-10-23
- **Initiative:** `03-code-graph`
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-lang-ruby/src/walk.rs` (`site_of`, `is_type_body`, `type_of_self`, `call`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-lang-ruby/tests/ruby.rs::a_call_in_a_type_body_is_made_by_the_type_on_itself`

A call in a class or module body outside every method runs as the type's body runs, as `track :size` does, so it is
the type's call, not one of the file-scope names of
[ADR 3.112](./3.112-keep-the-names-a-file-s-top-level-calls-and-uses.md). The type makes such a call, on itself, so
`track :size` binds the type's own `track`, at about 15 bytes of heap for each call site.

## Decision

- The innermost class or module whose body holds a call outside every method makes it, a call in a block of that body
  included.
- Such a call with no receiver, or on `self`, records the type's chain as its receiver, ending in `.` as a receiver
  on a class does ([ADR 3.15](./3.15-qualify-a-ruby-member-by-its-whole-namespace-chain.md)): `track :size` binds
  `Widget.track`.
- A macro the walk reads as structure ([ADR 3.15](./3.15-qualify-a-ruby-member-by-its-whole-namespace-chain.md)) is
  such a call too.
- A use the body makes through a symbol is the type's as well
  ([ADR 3.67](./3.67-record-a-function-a-string-or-a-symbol-names-as-a-use.md)).

## Why

- A class or module body runs with `self` as the type, so the type is the caller and the receiver.
- The pairs the type's receiver binds at confidence 60 and above are about 85% right; the pairs below, about 33%
  right, are the cascade's guesses that a method's call takes as well (Homebrew's library, 2026-10-23).

## Consequences

- About 63% of the new pairs are right overall, under the 90% a rule is taken at; every wrong one is a tier's guess
  for a runtime method on a literal or a constant, `[...].freeze` taking `Gem::Version#freeze`, or a namesake for a
  mixin's or a require's name, which the same call makes from a method too. Accepted.
- `dead_code` reports 226 fewer Ruby methods on Homebrew's library and adds none, and more methods turn uncertain,
  since a call that binds no definition takes the place of a file-scope name that reached every namesake. Accepted.
- Each call site takes the place of a file-scope name: about 15 bytes of heap each. Accepted.

## Rejected

- **A call with no receiver kept bare, with the type as its caller** — `track :size` binds no `Widget.track`, since the
  bare tiers look for the caller's owner's members and a type's owner is the type around it, and the 402 pairs at 85
  that the type's receiver adds, `register_scheme` among them, are lost.

## Revisit if

A sample of the pairs the type's receiver binds at 60 and above falls well under the 85% right measured here. Not
expected.
