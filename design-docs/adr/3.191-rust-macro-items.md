# Record the items a Rust macro of the file names by a metavariable

- **Date:** 2026-10-25
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-lang-rust/src/walk/macro_items.rs`, `crates/kartta-lang-rust/src/walk.rs`
  (`impl_target`), `crates/kartta-graph/src/resolve.rs` (`Cascade::resolve_call`, `binds_if_made`,
  `names_a_type_outside_the_graph`), `kartta_lang::SymbolFlags::is_made`
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-lang-rust/tests/extract.rs`
  (`an_item_a_macro_of_the_file_names_by_an_identifier_is_a_symbol_of_its_invocation`,
  `an_impl_a_macro_of_the_file_writes_for_a_type_it_binds_adds_the_trait_and_the_members`,
  `an_item_or_a_member_a_macro_of_the_file_makes_is_flagged_as_made`), `crates/kartta-languages/tests/engine.rs`
  (`a_rust_call_binds_a_definition_a_macro_writes_only_where_no_written_one_binds`,
  `a_rust_call_binds_no_value_a_macro_writes_and_reads_none_as_the_receiver_of_a_path`)

A `macro_rules!` whose transcriber writes `pub struct $name;` defines a type at each invocation, and the walk reads no
expansion, so the type was in no answer. The walk matches each invocation of such a macro of its own file against the
macro's arms and records what the matched arm names by a metavariable, and a call binds such an item only where no
written definition answers it, at the cost of what an arm makes any other way.

## Decision

- **Item macros.** A `macro_rules!` is an item macro where a transcriber of one of its arms writes `struct`, `union`,
  `enum`, `fn`, `const`, `static`, `trait`, `type` or `mod` before a metavariable, or an `impl .. { .. }` whose target
  is one metavariable. The reading skips each such item's body and descends into every other tree of the transcriber,
  a repetition's or a nested invocation's.
- **Matching.** An invocation by its bare name of an item macro the file defined before it takes the first arm whose
  pattern its tokens match whole. Each fragment is matched greedily and each repetition as often as its tokens allow,
  with no backtracking. An outer doc comment of the invocation stands for a pattern's `#` and its `[..]` tree.
- **Items.** The matched arm defines its items once for each identifier its metavariable binds, or each type of one
  identifier with its generic arguments, as a symbol of the item's kind on the identifier's line, a `union` a struct,
  qualified and owned where the invocation is, as a written item there is
  ([ADR 3.11](/design-docs/adr/3.11-rust-item-names.md)), and an item of the block a function body's invocation is in
  ([ADR 3.53](/design-docs/adr/3.53-rust-local-calls.md)). A `fn` with an owner is a method. The item is exported
  where the transcriber writes a `pub` before it, or a `$vis` bound to one, other than `pub(self)` and `pub(in self)`.
- **Impls.** An `impl` for `$name` makes the type it binds the owner of the impl's `fn`s, `const`s and `type`s, named
  as written or by each identifier a metavariable binds, as a written impl does, each on the line of the identifier
  the invocation binds to the type. `impl Trait for $name` adds `Trait` to the type's parents, with `$crate` spelled
  `crate` and the trait's generic arguments dropped.
- **Scope.** An item macro is in scope from its definition to the end of the block or module it is defined in, and a
  later `macro_rules!` of the name, one that names no item included, replaces it there.
- **Binding.** Each made item is flagged as made. A call the cascade binds to a made item, or leaves ambiguous where a
  made item shares its name, runs the cascade again without the made items and takes what that run binds; a member
  of the type the receiver is declared as binds in either run.
- **Confidence.** A call bound to a made item carries `confidence::MADE`, 55, where its tier is higher, so the
  `certain` floor leaves it out and `likely` keeps it, and binds nothing where its tier is below `SAME_DIR`.
- **Values.** A made constant or static binds no call, and counts as the receiver of a type path
  ([ADR 3.176](/design-docs/adr/3.176-rust-outside-type-calls.md)) only under a method call, so `BLACK` of
  `BLACK.to_rgba()` does and rust-analyzer's `sym::Box` of `Box::new()` does not.

## Why

- Item macros are common and simple: on the cargo registry, 83% of the 5,641 invocations of item macros of their own
  file match an arm that names its items (2026-10-24).
- A made item is a namesake of written definitions, `StateID::as_u32` beside `SmallIndex::as_u32`, and a written one
  is likelier the callee: bound after the written ones, all 30 sampled of the 141 pairs the made items remove on the
  registry are right (2026-10-25).
- A made item's name is often a trait's method or another file's made namesake, so a binding to one is weaker than its
  tier: 50 of 60 sampled of the 1,471 pairs added on the registry are right, and none of 12 sampled of those the
  parent-directory tier bound (2026-10-25).
- A made value is a list's entry, rust-analyzer's `define_symbols!` making `Some`, `Ok` and `Box`, so a call of its
  name means a namesake outside the graph.
- The reading costs nothing where a file defines no item macro: one empty-map check per invocation.

## Consequences

- An arm that forwards to another invocation defines nothing, as globset's `matches!` does to its own fuller arm: 958
  of the 5,641 registry invocations. Accepted.
- An invocation of a macro another file or crate defines defines nothing, and no answer says such items may be
  missing: 72,468 invocations at item position on the registry, `windows_targets::link!` and winapi's `STRUCT!`
  first (#610).
- An invocation that matches no arm, as a pattern the greedy reading takes wrongly, defines nothing: 12 of the 5,641.
  Accepted.
- A call of a name two made items share binds one by name as it would a written one, as proc-macro2's internal
  `Literal::u8_unsuffixed` for its public one: 104 of the 1,471 registry pairs, and on wasmtime 16 of 413 pairs bind
  pulley's `XReg::new` for another backend's. A method call on a value of a trait type binds a made namesake of the
  trait's method, as ciborium's `visitor.visit_u64(x)`. Accepted at `confidence::MADE`.
- The made items are stored as written ones are: a registry build's heap peaks 3.1 MB (1.1%) higher (2026-10-25).
  Accepted.
- The second run repeats the cascade for the calls it covers, with caches of its own for their names alone: with the
  made items' own extraction, a registry build and three Rust resolutions retire 3.7% more instructions (2026-10-25).
  Accepted.

## Rejected

- **Only an answer that says a macro may define items the graph does not hold** — leaves out what 83% of the
  invocations of a file's own item macros let the walk name.
- **The types without their impls' members** — a type the graph holds keeps every binding tier
  ([ADR 3.176](/design-docs/adr/3.176-rust-outside-type-calls.md)), so its `new` bound to the nearest namesake: 14 of
  the 17 pairs it added on tree-sitter were right.
- **Bind a made item as a written one** — a written namesake lost its call: 19 of 30 sampled of the 474 pairs it
  removed on the registry were right (2026-10-24).
- **Run every call of a name a macro makes without the made items first** — repeats the cascade for most calls of
  `new` or `clone`: 11% more instructions for a registry build and three resolutions, three to four times the cost of
  a second run only where a made item binds or leaves the call ambiguous, which binds no more right pairs
  (2026-10-25).
- **Keep a made binding at its tier** — the tier claims what the binding does not hold: none of 12 sampled registry
  pairs the parent-directory tier bound to a made item was right, and the 104 proc-macro2 pairs bind at the receiver
  tier.
- **Expand the matched arm and walk the result** — needs a token-level transcription the grammar's nodes cannot carry;
  the forwarding arms it would add are 958 of 5,641.

## Revisit if

The resolver gains a table of a crate's macros, so an invocation of another file's `macro_rules!` can be matched too.
