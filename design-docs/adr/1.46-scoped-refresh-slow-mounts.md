# Ask a slow mount about a named refresh's parts side by side

- **Date:** 2026-10-24
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-fs/src/index/scoped.rs` (`Index::parts_of`, `Rewalk::on_disk`, `Rewalk::read_rules`,
  `in_turn_then_side_by_side`, `side_by_side`, `WAITING_THREADS`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-fs/src/index/scoped.rs`
  (`the_asks_left_after_a_slow_one_wait_for_their_answers_at_once`,
  `asks_side_by_side_wait_on_at_most_the_threads_a_call_starts`,
  `parts_and_their_stats_asked_side_by_side_are_those_asked_one_after_another`,

`Index::refresh_paths` ([ADR 1.16](/design-docs/adr/1.16-scoped-refresh.md)) asks the filesystem about every part before
it walks one: whether each path's parent is still a directory, the name the disk holds, the part's own stat and the
rules of the directories above it. On a network mount each ask is a round trip, so asked one after another they cost a
call one round trip a path. A call asks in turn until one ask takes a round trip, then hands the rest to threads of its
own that wait for the answers side by side.

## Decision

- **What is asked side by side.** Three batches, one after another: the asks `Index::parts_of` makes while it works the
  parts out, the stat of a held directory a path's widening meets and the name of a part the index does not hold
  ([ADR 1.42](/design-docs/adr/1.42-refresh-path-spelling.md)); each part's own stat and name; and the rules of
  the directories above the parts on disk
  ([ADR 1.43](/design-docs/adr/1.43-scoped-refresh-rules.md)).
- **In turn, then side by side.** Each batch is asked on the calling thread one after another until one ask takes
  200 µs or more, longer than a local disk answers, and the rest go side by side. `parts_of` hands over whole paths:
  the path whose ask was slow finishes in turn, and it times its asks of the filesystem alone, not the lookups around
  them.
- **Threads of the call's own.** The rest are cut, in order, into at most 32 runs, each asked on a thread started for it
  and joined before the call goes on; the answers keep the order of the asks, so the parts, their stats, the rules and
  the first error a call returns are those asked in turn give. These threads wait for the filesystem; the index's
  threads (`BuildOptions::threads`) stay for what it computes. A single ask left, and a run whose thread does not
  start, are asked on the calling thread.
- **A run's own lookups.** A `parts_of` run starts from what the calling thread learnt and keeps its own lookups, so a
  directory that paths in several runs share is stat'd once by each.

## Why

- Asked in turn, a call over a network mount waited on each ask: 100 spread paths no call had named before took
  2,551 ms over NFSv3 with 1 ms added to each round trip, and 182 ms side by side; over SMB with 1 ms, 1,923 ms and
  165 ms (2026-10-24, #339).
- A mount answers as many asks at once as there are threads waiting on them: the stats of 100 parts took 1,398 ms in
  turn over NFSv3 with 1 ms, 223 ms on 8 threads and 73 ms on 32 (2026-10-24, #339).
- A local disk answers a stat in microseconds, faster than a thread starts, so a local call stays on the calling thread
  and costs what it cost in turn: 2.5 ms for 100 paths on the game-engine checkout either way (2026-10-24, #339).

## Consequences

- A call over a slow mount runs up to 32 threads beyond the index's, for as long as their round trips last; a call of
  100 paths over NFSv3 spent 49 ms of CPU over 182 ms (accepted).
- A local ask that takes past 200 µs, on a cold cache or a loaded machine, hands the rest of its batch to the threads
  (accepted).
- Each batch waits on its first slow ask in turn before it hands the rest over, and `parts_of` on the rest of that
  path's widening, one round trip for each held directory it climbs (accepted).

## Rejected

- **Read each parent directory in place of its parts' stats** — a listing asks about every entry: listing the 94
  parents of 100 spread paths took 125 s over NFSv3 and 48 s over SMB, each with 1 ms, against 1.4 s and 0.9 s for the
  parts' stats in turn.
- **Hand the asks to the index's threads** — overlaps as many as the index has threads: 859 ms for 100 paths over NFSv3
  on a two-thread index, against 125 ms on threads of the call's own.
- **16 or 64 waiting threads** — over NFSv3, 100 paths took 217 ms on 16 against 150 ms on 32; 64 took 103 ms, at
  42 ms of CPU against 31 ms and twice the threads, for a call that 32 already keeps under a fifth of a second.
- **Start the threads at the first ask** — spent a local call of 100 paths 8.5 ms of CPU against 4.3 ms.
- **Overlap the parts' own stats alone** — the asks of `parts_of` come first and stayed most of a call: 1,154 ms for
  100 paths over NFSv3.
- **Share `parts_of`'s lookups between its threads** — a lock on every lookup, to save repeated stats that the mount's
  client answers from its cache: 100 paths sent 986 RPCs over NFSv3 side by side, against 1,094 in turn.

## Revisit if

A platform answers a stat without a waiting thread, as an asynchronous call, or a mount's round trips grow so short that
asks in turn under 200 µs each add up to most of a call. Medium confidence: no such stat is in use, and the mounts
measured answered in 0.4 ms or more.
