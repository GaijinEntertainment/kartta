# Bound a Linux drain by the most events its queue can hold

A drain of [ADR 1.17](/design-docs/adr/1.17-change-notifications.md) hands over every change made before
the call, and a server publishes its index on that answer. An inotify queue can hold more than one drain reads in
500 ms, so a Linux drain reads on until it knows it has read the queue it found, bounded by the most events the queue
can hold, without asking the kernel for a count; the trade is that a deep queue lengthens the drain by the time its
reads take.

## Decision

- A drain reads one 64 KiB buffer a poll and does not close at 500 ms until either of two things holds: a read left
  room for the longest event, a header and a name of `PATH_MAX` bytes, so it found the queue empty; or the drain has
  read `fs.inotify.max_queued_events` events and one more, the overflow marker. Then it closes as a wait does.
- The limit is read from `/proc/sys/fs/inotify/max_queued_events` as the watcher starts, since an inotify instance takes
  the limit as it starts. Where it cannot be read, a drain reads until a read finds the queue empty.
- A batch closed before its reads covered what was queued is lost, as dropped changes are.
- A poll that a signal interrupts goes on for the time left of its timeout, in a drain and in a wait alike. On macOS and
  Windows a poll waits on a condition variable to its deadline whatever wakes the thread, and takes the whole queue at
  once, so a drain there closes as a wait does.

## Why

- inotify queues a change as the call that made it returns and hands its events over in order, and a read hands over
  every queued event that fits its buffer, so a short read or that many events read covers every change made before
  the drain, a dropped one included as the overflow marker.
- A drain that closed at 500 ms left the rest of a deep queue unread, the overflow marker with it, and the server
  published an index without those changes: a Docker VM's `max_queued_events` of 1,048,576 allows such a queue, where a
  distribution's 16,384 is read in eight polls.
- `FIONREAD` counts an inotify queue by walking it under the lock every writer into the watched tree takes to queue an
  event, so a count held each of those writers for the whole walk: 29.4–33.2 ms for a write beside a drain of 1,000,000
  queued notifications (2026-10-21).
- A poll cut short by a signal was taken for one that found nothing, so a drain whose reads had not covered its bound
  lost its batch, which costs a whole refresh under a new watcher.

## Consequences

- A deep queue costs a drain the time its reads take: a drain of 1,000,000 queued notifications took about a third of a
  second on 1.8 million entries (2026-10-21). Accepted.
- Writers faster than a drain reads can hold it open until it has read the queue's limit; beside 1 to 8 writers every
  drain closed just past 500 ms, each having overflowed the queue and handed over the whole tree. Accepted.

## Rejected

- **Close a drain at 500 ms and lose the batch when the queue is still unread** — every drain as short as a wait, but a
  queue it cannot read in time costs a whole refresh under a new watcher.
- **Count the queue through `FIONREAD` before reading** — stalls every writer into the tree for the count.
- **Count only when a drain reaches 500 ms before a read comes back short** — the drains that run longest still hold
  every writer for a walk of what is left, and the count stays a third state in every drain.
- **Read until a read finds the queue empty, with no bound** — nothing would close a drain whose writers outpace its
  reads, which the queue's limit does for one read of `/proc` at the start.

## Revisit if

The kernel offers a count of an inotify queue that takes no lock writers share. Low confidence that it will.
