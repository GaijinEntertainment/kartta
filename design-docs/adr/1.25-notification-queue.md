# Queue macOS and Windows notifications in a vector that grows

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Updated:** 2026-10-21
- **Initiative:** `01-kartta-fs-port`
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-fs/src/watch/notified.rs` (`QUEUED`, `Queue`, `Queuing`, `Backend::poll`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-fs/src/watch/notified.rs`
  (`a_queue_filled_to_its_bound_keeps_its_batch_and_room_for_the_next_once_read`,
  `more_notifications_than_the_queue_holds_while_the_build_walks_lose_the_batch`,
  `a_poll_once_the_notifications_stopped_coming_stops_the_watch`,

notify hands over macOS and Windows notifications on a thread of its own, and they wait for the caller of
[ADR 1.17](/design-docs/adr/1.17-change-notifications.md) while it walks or refreshes the tree. They wait in
a vector that grows as they arrive, bounded at 65,536, past which the batch is lost, as an overflowed inotify queue
loses its own; the trade is a lock that notify's thread and each poll share.

## Decision

- notify's handler turns each notification into a change or a loss, and pushes it onto a vector under a lock while
  fewer than 65,536 wait; past that it marks the queue overflowed, and the batch the next poll takes is lost.
- A poll waits on the vector's condition variable until a notification arrives or its deadline passes, whatever else
  wakes the thread, so a signal cannot cut it short. It takes the whole vector at once under the lock that checks the
  bound. A notification is dropped only when 65,536 wait, and a poll leaves unread none of those queued before it.
- The vector starts empty, so the queue takes no memory before the first notification arrives.
- When notify's handler is dropped, a poll that finds the vector empty reports `WatchError::Stopped`.

## Why

- A channel of 65,536 slots of 32 bytes is allocated and written as the watcher starts: 2,112 KiB resident before any
  notification arrived (2026-10-21), two thirds of a single-path start, and a tree that rarely changes never used most
  of it.
- notify does not deliver faster than a poll takes: the densest 1,000 consecutive events of 30,000 new files came over
  1.3 ms or more, where one poll takes 65,536 in about a millisecond (2026-10-21).
- An `FSEvents` burst can raise more notifications than the queue holds while a build walks, and how many it raises
  depends on the machine, so the overflow is tested on notify events made by hand.

## Consequences

- A full queue holds about 1.6 MiB. Accepted.
- A tree written across while the build walks, such as a data volume beside other builds, can overflow the queue, and
  the first drain then hands over the whole tree, which costs a whole refresh under a new watcher. Accepted.

## Rejected

- **Keep a channel of 65,536 slots made as the watcher starts** — no count beside the channel, but 2,112 KiB written at
  every start for room a tree that rarely changes never fills.
- **A channel that grows, bounded by a count of the notifications it holds** — no lock or condition variable, but the
  count and the channel are two states: between a poll's receipt of a notification and the count going down, a
  notification that meets the bound is dropped and the batch lost although the channel has room for it.

## Revisit if

The trees consumers follow overflow the queue during a build as a matter of course; a queue of one entry a path, not
one a notification, would then hold more. Medium confidence: only a data volume written across by other builds has.
