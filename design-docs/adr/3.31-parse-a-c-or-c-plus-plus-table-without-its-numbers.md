# Parse a C or C++ table without its numbers, and hold one tree at a time

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Revised:** 2026-10-22
- **Initiative:** `03-code-graph`

ADR 1.18's revision left the game-engine tree's build peak at its largest parse trees, which tree-sitter allocates
through the C library and a profiler of the Rust heap does not see. Counted through tree-sitter's allocator while each
file is extracted alone on macOS, the trees follow what a file holds, not its size. Of the tree's 145,000 claimed
files, 36 are of 1 MiB or more: 16 C and C++, 16 BLK, two JavaScript, one daslang, one Quirrel. The six C and C++ files
whose words are almost all numeric literals, a 30 MB table of network weights, 6.8 MB of shader bytes and four HRTF
sample tables of 2.4–5.2 MB, parse into 586, 299, 290, 267, 146 and 134 MB of trees and hold two definitions at most;
one call from another file reaches one of them. The largest trees of code are a 6.8 MB Vulkan header, 187 MB and 13,693
definitions that no other file calls, and miniaudio and duktape at 119 and 129 MB, with 133 and 8 callers outside.
Files that other files call most are smaller: imgui's 1.2 MB source has 4,018 callers outside it and holds 17.6 MB of
trees, `vulkan_core.h` 873 callers and 11 MB. A BLK file without an include is read without a tree, and the largest,
17 MB, costs nothing. The Go monorepo's 9 files of 1 MiB or more are Go of 1.1–1.8 MB, whose trees reach 41.5 MB.

**A brace group of 4 KiB or more that holds only numeric literals is blanked before the C++ parse.** The literals may
carry signs, suffixes, exponents and digit separators, between commas, whitespace, comments and nested groups of the
same; the group keeps its braces and every newline, so each line and byte offset outside it stays where it was, and the
extraction reads every name and signature from the original bytes. One scan judges each brace once: a group that turns
out to name something keeps the outermost all-numeric groups closed inside it. The six tables above parse into 0–0.3 MB
each. A table also took a whole file's definitions down: dav1d's `tables.c` declares a 193-row table through a
macro-wrapped declarator, a syntax error that tree-sitter could not recover from across the table, and its 20
definitions are extracted with the table blanked.

**The extraction frees the first tree before the macro repair's second parse** (ADR 3.3), which held both trees at
once: the Vulkan header peaks at 95 MB, miniaudio at 60 MB and duktape at 44 MB with its tables blanked.

## What it costs

Measured on Linux in a container limited to 4 CPUs (aarch64, glibc 2.41), cold engine builds of all sixteen languages
without a content cache, four interleaved rounds against `main`; the peak is `VmHWM`, resident is `VmRSS` after the
build:

| | game-engine tree | | | Go monorepo | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | peak | resident | build | peak | resident | build |
| `main` | 1,162–1,534 MB | 635–657 MB | 25.8–27.0 s | 585–626 MB | 355–368 MB | 10.0–10.9 s |
| this | 881–911 MB | 631–649 MB | 25.8–26.9 s | 591–625 MB | 356–367 MB | 10.1–10.4 s |

On macOS, three interleaved builds of each, the game-engine tree peaked at a footprint of 938–958 MB against
1,095–1,300 MB in 12.4–13.6 s against 12.6–14.3 s, and the monorepo at 635–682 MB against 620–678 MB in 3.4–4.0 s
against 3.4–3.7 s. The monorepo has no table to blank, and nothing of it moves beyond the spread.

The answers are `main`'s but for `tables.c`: the game-engine tree's C++ graph holds 1,266,664 definitions against
1,266,644 and one file fewer without any, and its call sites, their bindings and edges by family are `main`'s; the
monorepo's are `main`'s in every count. Every C and C++ file of the macOS data volume, 719,831 of them, extracts to the
same bytes as on `main` but the four copies of `tables.c`.

The game-engine tree's peak is no longer a parse tree: its largest left is the Vulkan header's 95 MB.

## Considered Options

Each measured as above, three interleaved rounds of the prototypes in one session:

- **Parse files of 1 MiB or more one at a time** — 972–1,158 MB against 1,102–1,202 MB for the same build without it,
  ranges that overlap, and 865–896 MB against 883–897 MB beside both changes here, within 2%: once the tables are
  blanked, the large trees left are rarely live at once, and a gate on them adds code for no measurable peak.
- **Leave a file above a size unparsed, held in the index and counted** — above 16 MiB, 898–1,052 MB, and the weight
  table, a 16 MB BLK file, 10 call sites and an edge lost; above 4 MiB, 876–908 MB, and nine files, 13,698 definitions,
  4,364 call sites, 23 bound sites and 14 edges lost. The peak is the blanking's, for answers lost.
- **Leave unparsed a file of 1 MiB or more whose first 64 KiB are 90% numeric literals** — 877–915 MB. Its six files on
  the game-engine tree are the six tables, and they take 9 definitions, 10 call sites and an edge with them.
- **Blank only, or free the first tree only** — 867–931 MB and 1,014–1,033 MB.
- **Blank groups of 64 bytes or more** — two more files of the game-engine tree gain their definitions, one each, and
  the peak is the same; each file with such a group pays a copy of its bytes, which was not measured.

Revisit for the generated parsers of tree-sitter grammars, which the macOS data volume holds by the dozen: their tables
spell symbols and macro calls, not bare numbers, so nothing here blanks them, and their trees reach 85–679 MB, the
largest 679 MB for a 30 MB `parser.c` that holds 31 definitions and 565,324 call sites of macros.

## Revised 2026-10-22: a generated parser's tables are blanked too

**In a parser tree-sitter generates for a grammar, a brace group of 4 KiB or more is blanked when it holds names,
macro calls, designators, strings and character literals beside what a numeric table holds.** Such a file is told by
its text before its first brace, which includes `tree_sitter/parser.h` and defines `LANGUAGE_VERSION`: each generated
`parser.c` of the data volume opens so, and a hand-written external scanner, which includes the same header, defines
no `LANGUAGE_VERSION`. The parse tables, the symbol names, map and metadata, the field maps, the lexer modes, the parse
actions, the reserved words, the character sets and the grammar's enumerations are blanked. The lexers and the
language function hold statements, so they are read as written, with the uses of the external scanner and of the
tables the language function names. A file is not told by its name: a language extracts bytes alone, and the content
cache keys an extraction by them (ADR 2.1).

The macOS data volume, 7.3–7.5 million entries and 918,000–962,000 C and C++ files while this was measured, holds 68
generated parsers of 25 grammars, in cargo's registry, Go's module cache and checkouts, and none under another name.
Counted through tree-sitter's allocator while each file is extracted alone, as above:

| parser | bytes | trees on `main` | blanked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| daslang | 30.2 MB | 678.4 MB | 2.0 MB |
| cpp | 16.5 MB | 371.2 MB | 6.4 MB |
| ruby | 14.6 MB | 351.9 MB | 3.3 MB |
| bash | 9.5 MB | 197.9 MB | 9.1 MB |
| tsx | 8.4 MB | 193.9 MB | 2.6 MB |
| markdown_inline | 2.2 MB | 45.3 MB | 8.4 MB |
| all 68 | 441.6 MB | 10,020.2 MB | 181.9 MB |

**Each generated parser holds at most 9.1 MB of trees**, where the 60 of 1 MiB or more held 20.7–24.9 times their
bytes on `main`. The trees left are the lexers', the code that makes every call inside the files' definitions: bash's
lexer of 10,400 lines and markdown_inline's of 10,200 hold the largest, and no parser of 1 MiB or more keeps more than
3.9 times its bytes.

The 68 files keep their answers. Each definition, callable and use inside a definition, and each call a definition
makes, extracts as on `main`, 1,831 definitions, 117,040 calls and 106,369 uses. Built cold on the eight roots that
hold 50 of the 68, cargo's registry, Go's tree-sitter modules, two daScript checkouts and four grammar repositories,
each language's graph holds `main`'s files, definitions by kind, call sites, uses and includes, and its calls bind as
on `main` at each confidence, with `main`'s edges and misses. What goes is what the tables spelled at file scope,
8,352,901 calls and 11,497,558 uses, which the graph keeps as names a file writes at file scope: 17,353 of the files'
24,272, counted once per file. They are 16,703 of the grammars' enumerators, nine macros of `parser.h`, `ACTIONS`,
`SHIFT`, `REDUCE`, `STATE` and their kin, in 59–67 files each, and `ts_builtin_sym_end` in 62, none a definition a
graph holds, and `TSStateId` in 8. The grammar reads the cast `(TSStateId)(-1)` in the lexer modes of those 8 as a
call, the only place they spell the typedef outside a declaration, so `find_callers` listed each as a file-scope caller
of it; it lists none now, as for every other cast.

Every other C and C++ file of the data volume extracts to the same bytes as on `main`: 961,360 files between a run of
`main` and one of the prototype, and 917,984 between that run of `main` and one of the change, the volume having lost
files between them; one of the 917,984, which another process rewrote between the runs, was extracted again alone on
each side. The test of whether a file is a generated parser takes 3.2 s summed over 918,118 files of 12,100 MB, of
which 1,074 MB come before a file's first brace: 0.2% of the 1,750 s of CPU a run that extracts them takes.

### What it costs

Measured on macOS, cold engine builds of all sixteen languages without a content cache, through a harness that
builds as the MCP server does and exits. The data volume was built by both sides at once, so that each pair reads the
same tree under the same load: three pairs of 3.87–3.89 million claimed files, 1.00 million of them C and C++, at load
averages of 29–52 from other work on the machine. The change builds in 936–1,014 s at a peak footprint of
23,318–25,723 MB, against 896–1,031 s and 23,540–24,896 MB on `main`, and the two sides of a pair differ by -1,273 to
+827 MB and -17 to +40 s, so neither moves beyond the spread of a build that size. With every allocation counted,
tree-sitter's too, in two more pairs of 4.02 million claimed files, the build's live heap peaks at 17,755–17,823 MB
against 17,813–17,870 MB, 48–58 MB lower within each pair, holds 16,343–16,348 MB after the build against
16,348–16,349 MB, and allocates 1,379.6 GB against 1,395.7–1,395.8 GB.

Cargo's registry, 33,680 entries with 850 C and C++ files and 23 of the parsers, builds in 2.3–5.0 s at a peak
footprint of 535–701 MB, against 4.7–7.6 s and 1,663–2,595 MB on `main`, six interleaved rounds at load averages of
30–49.

The game-engine tree and the Go monorepo hold no generated parser, and their answers are `main`'s to the call: none of
the parity plan's 21,096 and 24,238 calls differs. Built through the MCP server at load averages of 14–47, the
game-engine tree takes 13.4–16.3 s cold at a peak footprint of 1,034–1,211 MB, against 12.4–20.5 s and 1,126–1,212 MB
in six interleaved rounds, and 3.47–3.51 s warm at 890–934 MB, against 3.48–3.51 s and 912–944 MB. The monorepo takes
1.93–2.15 s cold at 396–526 MB, against 1.88–2.11 s and 434–546 MB in eight rounds, and 0.73–0.75 s warm at
326–379 MB, against 0.73–0.83 s and 311–377 MB in six. The change's only work on the monorepo is the scan of its 34 C
and C++ files' text before their first brace, which a warm build, extracting none of them, does not do either; through
the build-only harness it builds in 1.97–3.54 s at 408–557 MB, against 1.95–3.31 s and 425–487 MB in eight rounds, and
with every allocation counted, tree-sitter's too, it holds 219.7–220.0 MB after the build against 219.9–220.3 MB,
having allocated 9,842 MB either way, so a footprint gap there is the two binaries'.

### Considered Options

Each measured on the 68 parsers as above, behind a switch of one prototype, and over every C and C++ file of the data
volume where the rule reaches past them:

- **A brace group of 4 KiB or more of names, macro calls, designators, strings and numbers, in every file** — the 68
  parsers as here, and 1,639 other files extract otherwise: 40,293 calls and 2,527 uses inside definitions lost,
  131,802 names written at file scope lost, and where a blanked group changes how tree-sitter recovers from an error,
  118 definitions gained in 80 files and 4 lost in 2, with 3 callables fewer.
- **Every brace group of 4 KiB or more after `=` at file scope, whatever it holds** — the same extraction to the byte
  on each of the 68, 189 MB summed and 9.3 MB the largest, the enumerations parsed, and a scanner of its own for
  strings, characters and comments.
- **The same entries without strings and characters** — 216 MB summed and 9.4 MB the largest, with the symbol names
  and the character sets parsed; 16,765 names written at file scope kept beyond the change's, 16,703 enumerators and
  `ts_builtin_sym_end`, none a definition a graph holds.
- **Leave a generated parser unparsed** — no tree, and all 1,831 definitions, 117,040 calls and 106,369 uses inside
  definitions lost: the language function other files call, its uses of the external scanner, and the lexers.
- **Leave unparsed a file of 4 MiB or more whose banner announces generated code** — 4,516 MB summed and 371.7 MB the
  largest, since 20 of the 68 come from generators that wrote no banner, cpp's 16.5 MB parser among them; 643
  definitions, 35,716 calls and 35,483 uses inside definitions lost in the 68, and beyond them two copies of Perl's
  `charclass_invlists.h` unparsed, 31 definitions each.
- **Blank the lexers too** — not built: the trees left, at most 9.1 MB, are the lexers', which make all 117,040 calls,
  `set_contains` among them, a function `parser.h` defines.
