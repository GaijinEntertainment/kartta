# Parse a C or C++ table without its numbers, and hold one tree at a time

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Initiative:** `03-code-graph`

ADR 1.18's revision left the game-engine tree's build peak at its largest parse trees, which tree-sitter allocates
through the C library and a profiler of the Rust heap does not see. Counted through tree-sitter's allocator while each
file is extracted alone on macOS, the trees follow what a file holds, not its size. Of the tree's 145,000 claimed
files, 36 are of 1 MiB or more: 16 C and C++, 16 BLK, two JavaScript, one daslang, one Quirrel. The six C and C++ files
whose words are almost all numeric literals, a 30 MB table of network weights, 6.8 MB of shader bytes and four HRTF
sample tables of 2.4–5.2 MB, parse into 586, 299, 290, 267, 146 and 134 MB of trees and hold two definitions at most;
one call from another file reaches one of them. The largest trees of code are a 6.8 MB Vulkan header, 187 MB and 13,693
definitions that no other file calls, and miniaudio and duktape at 119 and 129 MB, with 133 and 8 callers outside.
Files that other files call most are smaller: imgui's 1.2 MB source has 4,018 callers outside it and holds 17.6 MB of
trees, `vulkan_core.h` 873 callers and 11 MB. A BLK file without an include is read without a tree, and the largest,
17 MB, costs nothing. The Go monorepo's 9 files of 1 MiB or more are Go of 1.1–1.8 MB, whose trees reach 41.5 MB.

**A brace group of 4 KiB or more that holds only numeric literals is blanked before the C++ parse.** The literals may
carry signs, suffixes, exponents and digit separators, between commas, whitespace, comments and nested groups of the
same; the group keeps its braces and every newline, so each line and byte offset outside it stays where it was, and the
extraction reads every name and signature from the original bytes. One scan judges each brace once: a group that turns
out to name something keeps the outermost all-numeric groups closed inside it. The six tables above parse into 0–0.3 MB
each. A table also took a whole file's definitions down: dav1d's `tables.c` declares a 193-row table through a
macro-wrapped declarator, a syntax error that tree-sitter could not recover from across the table, and its 20
definitions are extracted with the table blanked.

**The extraction frees the first tree before the macro repair's second parse** (ADR 3.3), which held both trees at
once: the Vulkan header peaks at 95 MB, miniaudio at 60 MB and duktape at 44 MB with its tables blanked.

## What it costs

Measured on Linux in a container limited to 4 CPUs (aarch64, glibc 2.41), cold engine builds of all sixteen languages
without a content cache, four interleaved rounds against `main`; the peak is `VmHWM`, resident is `VmRSS` after the
build:

| | game-engine tree | | | Go monorepo | | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | peak | resident | build | peak | resident | build |
| `main` | 1,162–1,534 MB | 635–657 MB | 25.8–27.0 s | 585–626 MB | 355–368 MB | 10.0–10.9 s |
| this | 881–911 MB | 631–649 MB | 25.8–26.9 s | 591–625 MB | 356–367 MB | 10.1–10.4 s |

On macOS, three interleaved builds of each, the game-engine tree peaked at a footprint of 938–958 MB against
1,095–1,300 MB in 12.4–13.6 s against 12.6–14.3 s, and the monorepo at 635–682 MB against 620–678 MB in 3.4–4.0 s
against 3.4–3.7 s. The monorepo has no table to blank, and nothing of it moves beyond the spread.

The answers are `main`'s but for `tables.c`: the game-engine tree's C++ graph holds 1,266,664 definitions against
1,266,644 and one file fewer without any, and its call sites, their bindings and edges by family are `main`'s; the
monorepo's are `main`'s in every count. Every C and C++ file of the macOS data volume, 719,831 of them, extracts to the
same bytes as on `main` but the four copies of `tables.c`.

The game-engine tree's peak is no longer a parse tree: its largest left is the Vulkan header's 95 MB.

## Considered Options

Each measured as above, three interleaved rounds of the prototypes in one session:

- **Parse files of 1 MiB or more one at a time** — 972–1,158 MB against 1,102–1,202 MB for the same build without it,
  ranges that overlap, and 865–896 MB against 883–897 MB beside both changes here, within 2%: once the tables are
  blanked, the large trees left are rarely live at once, and a gate on them adds code for no measurable peak.
- **Leave a file above a size unparsed, held in the index and counted** — above 16 MiB, 898–1,052 MB, and the weight
  table, a 16 MB BLK file, 10 call sites and an edge lost; above 4 MiB, 876–908 MB, and nine files, 13,698 definitions,
  4,364 call sites, 23 bound sites and 14 edges lost. The peak is the blanking's, for answers lost.
- **Leave unparsed a file of 1 MiB or more whose first 64 KiB are 90% numeric literals** — 877–915 MB. Its six files on
  the game-engine tree are the six tables, and they take 9 definitions, 10 call sites and an edge with them.
- **Blank only, or free the first tree only** — 867–931 MB and 1,014–1,033 MB.
- **Blank groups of 64 bytes or more** — two more files of the game-engine tree gain their definitions, one each, and
  the peak is the same; each file with such a group pays a copy of its bytes, which was not measured.

Revisit for the generated parsers of tree-sitter grammars, which the macOS data volume holds by the dozen: their tables
spell symbols and macro calls, not bare numbers, so nothing here blanks them, and their trees reach 85–679 MB, the
largest 679 MB for a 30 MB `parser.c` that holds 31 definitions and 565,324 call sites of macros.
