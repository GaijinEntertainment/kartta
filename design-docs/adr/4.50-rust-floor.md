# Hold every published crate to one Rust floor that trails stable

- **Date:** 2026-11-02
- **Governs:** `Cargo.toml` (`workspace.package.rust-version`), `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (job `msrv`)
- **Pinned by:** `.github/workflows/ci.yml` (job `msrv`)

Cargo refuses a crate whose `rust-version` is above the consumer's toolchain, so a floor at the pinned stable shuts out
everyone a release behind for code that does not need it. The workspace states what its published crates need, on one
floor that a pinned toolchain builds; the trade is that the smallest crates carry the floor of the largest.

## Decision

- **The floor.** Every crate inherits one `rust-version`, 1.92, the lowest toolchain that builds all the published
  crates. The pinned toolchain of `rust-toolchain.toml` stays free to be newer: it builds and lints the repository, and
  the floor is what a consumer needs.
- **What raises it.** The floor rises only when a dependency or a library symbol needs a newer toolchain and no short
  rewrite avoids it, and to no toolchain released less than six months earlier. A dependency that needs a younger one
  waits, unless an advisory forces the bump, and the raise names the advisory.
- **How it lands.** A raise is a commit of its own that moves the field and the job's pin together, names what forced
  it, and ships in a minor release, never a patch.
- **The job.** The `msrv` job runs `cargo check --locked` over every published crate on the pinned floor toolchain, on
  Linux, macOS and Windows. It covers what a consumer builds, so tests and dev-dependencies may use newer toolchains.

## Why

- A scratch consumer on 1.97.0, two releases behind, stopped at `kartta-fuzz@0.0.0 requires rustc 1.99` with the floor
  at stable, though that crate builds on 1.85.0 (2026-11-02).
- The published crates built on 1.97.0, and two `bit_width` calls alone held them above 1.92; their replacement is that
  method's own body. `Box::new_zeroed_slice` is what 1.91.1 refuses.
- `tree-sitter` 0.27.1 declares 1.90, so the language crates cannot go below that.
- Tokio raises its own floor only to a toolchain six months old and only in a minor release (its README), and a raise
  in a minor is safe because cargo's Rust-aware resolver then keeps an older toolchain on the previous version.
- Moving the field from 1.99 to 1.92 holds back no dependency: `cargo update --dry-run` lists the same 27 moves on both.

## Consequences

- `kartta-fuzz` builds on 1.85 and `kartta-walk` on 1.87, and each declares 1.92. A consumer of either alone on an older
  toolchain must upgrade. Accepted.
- A consumer with no lockfile and a resolver that ignores `rust-version` takes the newest dependencies, and a later
  dependency release can break that build on the floor. A fresh edition-2021 consumer of `kartta` built on 1.92.0
  (2026-11-02). Accepted.
- A raise edits two sites, and the job fails until both agree. Accepted.

## Rejected

- **The floor at the pinned stable** — the refusal above, for no symbol the crates need.
- **One floor per crate** — one crate at 1.85 sets the whole workspace's resolver to Rust 1.85: `cargo update --dry-run`
  then downgrades `encoding_rs` and `uuid` in the lockfile (2026-11-02).
- **1.91** — it gains one release and costs a rewrite of the zeroed per-entry tables in `kartta-fs`.
- **A floor a fixed count of releases behind stable** — it rises on the calendar, with nothing needing the newer
  toolchain.
- **A job that reads its toolchain from the field** — a raised field then passes unseen, so the floor moves with no
  deliberate step.

## Revisit if

`tree-sitter` raises its floor past the six-month bound, a consumer needs an older toolchain, or cargo resolves each
workspace member against its own `rust-version`, which would make per-crate floors free. High confidence in the rule;
medium in the six months, which follows Tokio's practice rather than a measured need.
