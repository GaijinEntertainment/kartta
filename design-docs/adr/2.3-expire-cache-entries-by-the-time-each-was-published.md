# Expire cache entries by the time each was published

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Revised:** 2026-10-19
- **Initiative:** `02-shared-cache`

ADR 2.1 built a store nothing ever leaves. Entries for contents no tree holds any more stay, every entry of a namespace
an older fingerprint wrote stays — every entry of a language after a grammar or extractor change — and so do the
temporary files of writers killed before they published. A node that runs review containers against one shared cache
for weeks fills its disk. Packs are never rewritten in place and a merge moves entries into a new pack, so a pack file's
time says nothing about its entries' age: expiry needs an age per entry that survives a merge.

**Each index entry carries the second it was published, and the merge removes the entries older than a time to live.**
An entry record grows from 52 to 56 bytes with a `u32` of seconds since the Unix epoch, which lasts until 2106, and the
pack format becomes version 2. The time is the moment the writer was made — for a build, when it began — and a merge
copies it with the entry, so an entry's age counts from when it was computed, however many merges move it. An age
counts from the start of the recorded second, so the merge rounds its cutoff up and no entry outlives its time to live.
A lookup does not read the time and does not renew it: an entry every build finds expires once per time to live, and
the first build after its pack is rewritten without it computes it again, at most one recomputation per entry per
window, and no lookup ever writes. The check of ADR 2.1 does not cover the time: a damaged time makes an entry expire
early or late, which costs a recomputation or some disk and never serves wrong data.

**A pack's name leaves the times out.** ADR 2.1 names a pack by a hash over its index, so that two writers publishing
the same entries over the same payloads name one pack and the second finds it already in place; the name hashes each
record without its time, so writers that publish at different seconds still share one file. The entries of a publish
that finds its pack in place keep that file's times, so they expire earlier than the publish's own times would make
them, by at most the time between the two publishes. That costs at most one extra extraction per entry, never a wrong
answer: the payloads are identical and content-addressed.

**A pack is rewritten without its expired entries once they hold half of its payload bytes.** The merge of ADR 2.1
leaves the largest pack alone while the others hold fewer entries, so expiry adds its own reason to merge a pack:
whatever the count of packs, a pack whose expired entries hold half of its payload bytes or more is merged, and every
pack the merge takes loses its expired entries. A pack whose every entry expired is removed without being read, since
the merge checks only the entries it keeps. Until a pack passes half, a lookup still finds its expired entries, which
serves nothing wrong — an entry's key names exactly what it was computed from — and treating them as misses would
publish second copies beside them. After a merge, a pack's expired entries hold fewer payload bytes than its unexpired
ones, so a store's payloads take at most about twice what its unexpired entries' payloads take — unless a pack the
merge rewrote could not be removed, which stays beside its rewrite and is counted in `Merged::left_in_place` until a
later merge removes it. The bound counts payload bytes only: the 56-byte index record of every expired entry a pack
keeps comes on top, and a pack whose expired entries are many and small keeps their records until a merge takes it.
The merge counts the expired entries of every pack from the index alone, without reading a payload.

**Every merge expires, with the time to live its caller gives, and the default is a week.** A build ends with a merge
(ADR 2.2), so expiry runs once per build; counting what aged costs under a millisecond, and nothing turns it off.
`BuildOptions::content_cache_ttl` carries the time to live to the build and its refreshes, the FFI build params and the
Node and Go wire types take it as `content_cache_ttl_secs`, and the MCP server reads `KARTTA_CACHE_ENTRY_TTL_SECS`
(ADR 4.1). A time to live of zero from any of them is the week, never "expire everything": zero is what an unset number
decodes to, and the two readings differ by a whole shared cache. `kartta_cache::Store::merge` itself takes the duration
as given. A week recomputes each live entry at most once a week, where a cold build of the game-engine checkout takes
13.4–14.9s against 3.7–4.0s warm. An entry of contents that left every tree expires a week after it was published, and
leaves the store only once its pack is rewritten or removed; the rewrite starts once the pack's expired entries hold
half of its payload bytes, so an expired entry can stay well past the week, within the bound above. The deployments the
MCP server answers set `KARTTA_CACHE_SWEEP` to turn a sweep on; with expiry at every merge there is nothing for it to
switch, so the server does not read it. Revised 2026-10-19: a value no longer warns that it does not apply. The sweep it
asks for runs at every merge, so the warning gave a deployment nothing to act on, and no deployment relies on it: the
server was never released.

**The merge also removes what writers and older formats leave behind.** A temporary file goes once it has been unwritten
for an hour and no writer holds its lock: a writer takes an exclusive lock on its file when it creates it, and a killed
process drops its locks, so a build that writes rarely for an hour keeps its file. On a filesystem without locks the
lock is not taken, and a build that leaves its file unwritten for an hour can lose it to another merge, which costs
that build's publish and nothing else. A file with the pack extension the store did not map — of format version 1,
damaged, foreign, or published after the store opened — goes once its file is older than both the time to live and an
hour, since every entry in it was published before its file was last written; two versions sharing a directory during a
rollout therefore never remove each other's fresh packs. The hour holds whatever the time to live: a pack another
process published after the store opened is one the store did not map, and a merge given a time to live under an hour,
zero included, would otherwise remove it as it lands.

**What a merge did is its answer.** `Merged` counts the entries it removed by age, the expired entries it left — in
packs under half expired, or in merged packs it could not remove — the temporary files and unreadable packs it removed,
and those it could not remove. `Store::stats` reports no age: only a merge holds a time to live, and an oldest time
would cost a scan of every index on each call.

**A build reports what its merge could not remove.** The merge a build or a refresh ends with answers `Merged`, and
the files it could not remove — merged packs left in place, which hold their entries twice, temporary files and
unmapped packs — are a `BuildError::Cache` naming each count, so they reach every consumer through the errors it already
reads. What the merge expired, and the expired entries it kept, stay `Merged`'s own: they are the store working as
designed, and a count on every build outcome and answer would tell an agent nothing it acts on. Revised 2026-10-19:
the build dropped `Merged`, so a directory whose files the merge could not remove grew with nothing said.

## What it costs

Measured on macOS with 18 cores, on the game-engine checkout, with a store a build with the content index and every
language filled: 278,678 entries, trigram sets and graph extractions.

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pack | 544,761,603 bytes | 545,876,315 bytes, 4 bytes an entry |
| warm build, content index in memory, every language | 3.67–4.03s | 3.65–3.80s |
| peak footprint of that build | 925–1000 MB | 947–1030 MB |
| every key looked up, one thread, one pack | 410–431ms | 410–431ms |
| as many absent keys | 14.5–16.9ms | 14.6–17.0ms |

The footprint ranges, over twelve runs before and nine after, overlap. The merge every build ends with, timed on one
pack holding every entry with a share of them planted eight days old against the week:

| entries past the time to live | the merge | time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| none | counts | 0.83–0.89ms |
| 10% | counts, leaves the pack | 0.73–0.84ms |
| 45% | counts, leaves the pack | 0.72–0.81ms |
| 60% | rewrites the pack with 111,471 entries | 233–234ms |
| every one | removes the pack | 1.3–1.7ms |
| the pack and eight small ones, 29% | merges the eight, 5,574 entries | 18–20ms |
| the pack and eight small ones, 58% | merges all nine into 117,045 entries | 362ms |

Checking every entry a merge read, expired or not, took 483ms for the 60% rewrite and 432ms to remove a pack every
entry of which had expired; checking only the entries kept gave the times above.

## Considered Options

- **A time per pack, with packs grouped into age generations and a whole generation removed at once** — expiry with no
  rewrite, but a merge confined to a generation keeps at least one pack per generation, eight for a week in days, and
  every miss searches them all. On the same entries spread over more packs, the absent keys took 87ms over nine packs,
  144ms over sixteen and 283–314ms over thirty-two, against 15ms over one, and every key found 439–456ms over sixteen
  and 508–528ms over thirty-two. Its saving over per-entry times is the rewrite of a pack past half expired, 0.23s once
  per pack per crossing: a pack that expires whole is removed in under 2ms either way.
- **The times in a column of their own after the index** — lookups take the same time over 56-byte records as over
  52-byte ones, so a column would add a section and its offsets to the format for nothing.
- **The oldest and newest time of a pack in its footer** — would spare the count, which takes under a millisecond over
  278,678 entries.
- **Renewing an entry when a lookup finds it** — a pack is never written again, so renewal means publishing every found
  entry again: a warm build would copy its whole store.
- **Treating an expired entry as a miss** — the next build publishes a second copy while the first waits for its pack's
  rewrite, and the expired one was never wrong.
- **Rewriting a pack for any expired entry** — once the oldest entry of the largest pack expires, every build would
  rewrite it, 0.23s or more each.
- **A rewrite share counted in entries** — the disk is what fills, and graph extractions vary in size far more than
  entries in count.
- **Expiry only when a caller asks, off by default** — the store then grows without bound wherever nobody asks, and
  asking would save under a millisecond a build.
- **Removing packs of another format at once** — two versions sharing a directory during a rollout would remove each
  other's packs.
- **An hour of age alone for a temporary file** — a build that inserts little for an hour would lose its file to another
  process's merge and its publish with it.
- **The time to live alone for a pack the store did not map** — a merge given a time to live under an hour would remove
  a pack another process published after the store opened, before any build read it.
- **The publication times in a pack's name** — a publish that finds its entries in place would write them again with
  its own times, but two identical publishes at different seconds, concurrent ones included, would leave two packs
  where ADR 2.1 shares one; the times a shared name keeps cost at most one early recomputation per entry.

Revisit when a store fills its disk within one time to live, which a shorter time to live or a lower rewrite share would
answer; when rewriting a pack past half expired costs a build more than recomputing what the pack held; or before 2106.
