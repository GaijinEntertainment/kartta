# Select a name whose last part holds a separator by its longer parts

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Updated:** 2026-10-21
- **Initiative:** `04-consumer-surfaces`
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-graph/src/graph.rs` (`Graph::definitions`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-mcp/tests/names.rs`
  (`a_qualified_name_whose_last_part_holds_a_separator_is_asked_back_as_the_outline_prints_it`)

A qualified name selects among the definitions named by its part after its last separator, and a definition whose own
name holds a separator, C++'s `operator/`, daslang's `operator . nineGrid :=`, JavaScript's `[Symbol.toStringTag]`,
TypeScript's `#handle` and `'/'`, is named by no such part. A name that selects nothing by its last part is read by each
longer part after a separator, so the `qn=` an outline prints selects its definition, at a few lookups only a miss pays.

## Decision

- A name that selects nothing as written and by its last part is read by each longer part after a separator, the
  shortest first, and selects as the last part does: `das::operator/` the C++ operator, `Kartta.#handle` the field.
- A name that selects as written or by its last part is read by no longer part.
- No answer changes its shape, and the C ABI, which selects through the same lookup, answers these names alike.
- A callee written with such a qualified name is listed and bound under that name's own last part
  ([ADR 3.38](/design-docs/adr/3.38-callee-listing-key.md)).

## Why

- Every symbol tool answered "no symbol matches" for the `qn=` an outline gives such a definition, and the alternatives
  left it out, since its qualified name is the name asked: 2,006 names on the C++ game-engine tree, counted from its own
  outlines (2026-10-21).
- A name that selects as written or by its last part pays one lookup of each. Only a miss tries the longer parts, a
  lookup per further separator and a few tens of nanoseconds before the alternatives search that a miss runs anyway.

## Consequences

- None measured: each of those names selects its own definition on the three trees, and every name that selected before
  answers as it did.

## Rejected

- **Reading every longer part of every name** — a name that selects by its last part pays a lookup for each further
  separator.
- **Keeping the names that hold a separator in an index under their last part** — a second map the graph updates on
  every insert, and those names' selection took three times as long.

## Revisit if

A language's definitions hold separators often enough that a miss's longer-part lookups show in a profile of a symbol
query.
