# Record a Ruby attribute write as a call of its setter

- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-lang-ruby/src/walk.rs` (`call`), `crates/kartta-lang-ruby/src/lib.rs` (`setter_suffix`),
  `crates/kartta-graph/src/resolve.rs` (`writes_on_a_value`), `kartta_lang::Conventions::setter_suffix`
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-lang-ruby/tests/ruby.rs` (`every_attribute_write_target_calls_the_setter_alone`,
  `a_constant_attribute_write_calls_the_setter_of_the_constant_name`,
  `an_operator_write_calls_the_reader_before_the_setter`, `only_the_written_attribute_of_a_chain_is_a_setter`),
  `crates/kartta-languages/tests/ruby_attribute_writes.rs`,
  `crates/kartta-graph/tests/graph/calls.rs` (`an_attribute_write_on_a_value_binds_by_name_only_as_a_guess`)

`a.b = c` reads like a call of `b` and is a call of `b=`, so the extraction records the name Ruby sends, and the reader
keeps only the callers that read it. A setter on a value the graph cannot type binds by name alone and is wrong too
often to read as more than a guess, which keeps the edge at the cost of a low tier.

## Decision

- **A write target calls the setter alone.** A call in a target of a write, an assignment, a list or a splat of one,
  `for` and `rescue =>`, records `b=` with the receiver it writes, and no call of `b`. A constant name after a `.`,
  `a.B = 1`, is `B=`; `a::B = 1` writes a constant, and calls nothing
  ([ADR 3.15](/design-docs/adr/3.15-ruby-member-names.md)).
- **An operator write calls the reader and the setter.** `a.b += c`, `a.b ||= c` and `a.b &&= c` record `b`, then `b=`,
  on one receiver. `||=` and `&&=` can skip the setter at run time, and the call stands for what the source may run.
- **A setter on a value binds only as a guess.** Where a language names `Conventions::setter_suffix`, a call of a method
  with that suffix after an alphanumeric or `_` on an empty or lower-case receiver binds at `FIRST_PARTY`, whatever tier
  found it. A receiver that names a type, `self` as the caller's chain or a constant, keeps its tier.
- **An index write records nothing.** `a[1] = 2` is a call of `[]=`, and `a[1] += 2` reads `[]` first and then writes
  `[]=`, as the walk leaves every `[]` unrecorded, so it binds neither.

## Why

- The parser builds `b=` for every target: `attrset` (`parse.y:13717`, Ruby 3.4.12) sets `rb_id_attrset` over the
  `NODE_ATTRASGN` of `lhs` and `mlhs_node` (`parse.y:3647`, `3692`), which `for` and `rescue =>` take too
  (`parse.y:4926`, `5964`), and `&.` keeps the bare name there to take the same setter at compile time
  (`compile.c:10253`). `compile_op_asgn2` (`compile.c:9575`) sends the reader, then the setter. `aryset`
  (`parse.y:13702`) sends `[]=`, and `compile_op_asgn1` (`compile.c:9441`) sends `[]` first.
- On Homebrew's library, Rails and RuboCop the written reader was false: 60 of 60 sampled removed pairs read as a write
  alone (2026-11-01, #871).
- A setter on a type's receiver reads right, 58 of 60 sampled, and one on a value right 47 of 60 over all writes, about
  19 of 30 among those on a value alone (2026-11-01, #871).

## Consequences

- A right setter on a value drops out under any `min_confidence` above `FIRST_PARTY`: 3,840 of the 7,774 pairs added on
  the three trees bind there. Accepted, because the default floor still shows them, labelled.
- A read on a value keeps the name tiers, so a reader on `x.y` can outrank the setter on `x.y = 1` of the same type.
  Accepted.
- A `self.x = 1` that binds no member of the caller's chain still takes a name tier: `self.key = str` in
  `OpenSSL::Cipher`, whose setter is native, binds `Net::HTTP#key=`. Accepted.
- An index write is no edge to a `def []=`, of which the three trees hold 44, across about 6,300 lines that write by
  index. Accepted until `[]` itself is recorded.

## Rejected

- **Bind a setter on a value by the name tiers** — keeps every pair at its tier, but only 47 of 60 sampled added pairs
  read right, under the 90% bar.
- **Record no call for a write on a value** — binds nothing false, but leaves the setter blind to 3,840 pairs on the
  three trees, about 60% of them right.
- **Keep the reader beside the setter on a plain write** — keeps the callers `def b` has, each of them false, as the
  parser sends no reader.

## Revisit if

The graph follows the type of a value in Ruby, which would let a setter on one bind by its type. Not planned.
