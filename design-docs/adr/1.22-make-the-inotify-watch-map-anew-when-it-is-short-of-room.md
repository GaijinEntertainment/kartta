# Make the inotify watch map anew when it is short of room

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Updated:** 2026-10-21
- **Initiative:** `01-kartta-fs-port`
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-fs/src/watch/inotify.rs` (`Walked::into_dirs`, `Backend::release`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-fs/src/watch/inotify.rs`
  (`a_watched_directory_deleted_and_made_again_leaves_the_watch_map_its_size`,
  `a_watched_directory_moved_away_and_back_leaves_the_watch_map_its_size`,

The Linux watcher of [ADR 1.20](./1.20-watch-on-linux-each-directory-the-index-keeps-from-the-walk-that-keeps-it.md)
keeps a hash map from each watch to its directory's path, and a followed tree takes directories out of it and puts as
many back. The map is made anew at the size it needs whenever a walk's watches do not fit the room left, and a release
shrinks it, so a cycle of removals and additions leaves it its size; the trade is a pass over the map at each such
walk.

## Decision

- The watches a walk took go into the map once the walk ends. When the room the map has left is short of them, a map
  with room for the watches it holds and the new ones is made, and both go into it.
- A release that removed any watch shrinks the map to the size the watches it keeps need.
- The notice of a released watch removes that watch alone, and nothing is added for it.

## Why

- A removal leaves a tombstone wherever the slots around it are full, and the tombstone keeps the room the entry held,
  so a `reserve` past the room left rehashed a table over half full into one of twice the buckets, even where the
  watches fit once the tombstones were gone. A directory deleted and made again, moved out of the tree and back, or
  hidden and shown again doubled the map: 60.94 MiB of heap after such cycles against the 56.81 MiB the build left, on
  1.8 million entries and 111,001 watches (2026-10-21).
- A shrink resizes only to fewer buckets, so it clears no tombstone while the watches it keeps need the buckets there
  are; making the map anew does, at the cost of the pass the doubling rehash took too.

## Consequences

- A walk whose watches do not fit pays a pass over the map, as a doubling rehash does; the refresh that took a deleted
  copy of 11,100 directories back in took a median of 85.6 ms (2026-10-21). Accepted.

## Rejected

- **Shrink the map after each read that removes a watch** — gives a doubled table back once its watches fit half the
  buckets, but clears no tombstone, so a deletion, whose notices come over many reads, ends every cycle doubled.
- **Make the map anew and drop the shrink after a release** — the same heap over every cycle measured, but a release of
  most of the watches would keep their buckets until a walk outgrew the room left.

## Revisit if

The standard library's hash map stops leaving tombstones that keep room. Low confidence that it will.
