# Expire cache entries by the time each was published

- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-cache/src/pack.rs` (`Published`, `expired_before`, `entries_since`),
  `kartta_cache::Store::merge`, `kartta_cache::DEFAULT_TTL`, `kartta_cache::Merged` (`expired`, `expired_kept`),
  `kartta_fs::BuildOptions::content_cache_ttl`
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-cache/src/store.rs`
  (`a_pack_whose_every_entry_expired_is_removed_and_its_entries_counted`,
  `a_pack_whose_expired_entries_hold_half_its_bytes_is_rewritten_without_them`,
  `a_rewritten_pack_keeps_each_entry_s_publication_time`,
  `expired_entries_under_half_a_pack_s_bytes_are_counted_and_still_found`,
  `an_entry_expires_once_more_than_a_ttl_with_a_fraction_of_a_second_has_passed`,
  `the_same_entries_published_again_are_one_pack_aging_from_the_first_publish`), `crates/kartta-fs/tests/cache.rs`
  (`a_build_removes_what_is_older_than_the_time_to_live_it_names`,
  `a_refresh_removes_what_is_older_than_the_build_s_time_to_live`,
  `a_time_to_live_of_zero_is_a_week_and_never_empties_the_store`)

A store of [ADR 2.1](/design-docs/adr/2.1-shared-cache-store.md) keeps every entry it was given, so
a node whose builds share one cache for weeks fills its disk with contents no tree holds and namespaces no producer
computes. Each entry carries the second it was published, and every merge drops what is older than a time to live, a
week by default; a pack is rewritten only once its expired entries hold half of its payload bytes, so a store may hold
about twice what is live.

## Decision

- **The time.** Each index entry carries the second it was published, which is the second its writer was made, for a
  build the second it began. A merge copies it with the entry, so an entry's age counts from the build that computed it
  however many merges move it, and no entry outlives its time to live.
- **Lookups.** A lookup does not read the time and does not renew it, and no lookup writes. An entry every build finds
  expires once per time to live, and the first build after its pack is rewritten without it computes it again. The
  check of [ADR 2.1](/design-docs/adr/2.1-shared-cache-store.md) does not cover the time: a damaged time makes an
  entry expire early or late and never serves wrong data.
- **Names.** A pack's name hashes each record without its time, so writers that publish the same entries at different
  seconds still share one file.
- **The rewrite.** Whatever the count of packs
  ([ADR 2.4](/design-docs/adr/2.4-cache-pack-merge.md)), a merge takes a pack whose expired entries hold
  half of its payload bytes or more, and every pack a merge takes loses its expired entries. Until a pack passes half,
  lookups still find its expired entries.
- **The time to live.** Every merge expires, with the time to live its caller gives, and nothing turns it off; a build
  ends with a merge ([ADR 2.2](/design-docs/adr/2.2-build-cache-lookup.md)), so expiry runs once
  per build. `kartta_cache::DEFAULT_TTL` is a week. `BuildOptions::content_cache_ttl`, the `content_cache_ttl_secs` of
  the FFI build params and of the Node and Go wire types, and the MCP server's `KARTTA_CACHE_ENTRY_TTL_SECS` carry the
  time to live to a build and its refreshes, and a zero from any of them is the week, never "expire everything".
  `Store::merge` itself takes the duration as given, so a zero there expires every entry whose second began before the
  merge.
- **No sweep switch.** The MCP server does not read `KARTTA_CACHE_SWEEP`, which its deployments set to ask for a sweep:
  every merge already expires.
- **What a merge says.** `Merged` counts the entries a merge removed by age and the expired entries it left.
  `Store::stats` reports no age.
- **Leftover files.** [ADR 2.5](/design-docs/adr/2.5-cache-leftover-files.md) removes the files writers and older
  formats leave behind, and reports what a build's merge could not remove.

[`Store::merge`](/crates/kartta-cache/src/store.rs), [`PackWriter`](/crates/kartta-cache/src/writer.rs) and
[`Merged`](/crates/kartta-cache/src/store.rs) state every case: the second an entry records and how the merge rounds
it, a publish that finds its pack in place, a pack removed unread once every entry expired, and what each count holds.

## Why

- A merge moves entries into a new pack, so a pack file's time says nothing about its entries' age: expiry needs an age
  per entry that survives a merge. The 4 bytes left a warm build and every lookup unchanged on the C++ game-engine
  tree's 278,678 entries (macOS, 2026-10-18, #81).
- Zero is what an unset number decodes to, and reading it as "expire everything" would empty a cache other builds share.
- A week recomputes each live entry at most once a week, where a cold build of the game-engine checkout took 13.4-14.9 s
  against 3.7-4.0 s warm (2026-10-18, #81).
- Counting what aged costs under a millisecond a merge, and rewriting a pack 60% expired took 233-234 ms, so the half
  share keeps rewrites rare (#81). Checking only the entries kept is what lets a pack whose every entry expired go in
  under 2 ms, where checking every entry it read took 432 ms.
- An expired entry is never wrong, since its key names exactly what it was computed from, so finding it serves nothing
  wrong.
- Only a merge holds a time to live, and an oldest time in `Store::stats` would cost a scan of every index on each call.

## Consequences

- Payloads take at most about twice what the unexpired entries' payloads take, plus the 56-byte record of every expired
  entry a pack keeps and any merged pack that could not be removed. Accepted.
- An entry of contents that left every tree leaves the store only when its pack is rewritten or removed, which can be
  well past the week. Accepted.
- An entry that every build finds is computed again once per time to live, and a publish that met its pack in place
  ages from that pack's times: at most one extra extraction per entry per window. Accepted.
- The `u32` of seconds runs out in 2106. Accepted.

## Rejected

- **A time per pack, with packs grouped into age generations** — expiry with no rewrite, but a merge confined to a
  generation keeps a pack per generation, eight for a week in days, and every miss searches them all: absent keys took
  283-314 ms over thirty-two packs against 15 ms over one. Its saving is the 0.23 s rewrite of a pack past half.
- **The times in a column of their own after the index** — lookups take the same time over 56-byte records as over
  52-byte ones, so a column would add a section to the format for nothing.
- **The oldest and newest time of a pack in its footer** — would spare a count that takes under a millisecond.
- **Renewing an entry when a lookup finds it** — a pack is never written again, so a warm build would publish its whole
  store again.
- **Treating an expired entry as a miss** — the next build publishes a second copy while the first waits for its pack's
  rewrite, and the expired one was never wrong.
- **Rewriting a pack for any expired entry** — once the largest pack's oldest entry expires, every build rewrites it,
  0.23 s or more each.
- **A rewrite share counted in entries** — the disk is what fills, and graph extractions vary in size far more than in
  count.
- **Expiry only when a caller asks, off by default** — the store grows without bound wherever nobody asks, to save under
  a millisecond a build.
- **The publication times in a pack's name** — two identical publishes at different seconds would leave two packs where
  [ADR 2.1](/design-docs/adr/2.1-shared-cache-store.md) shares one.

## Revisit if

A store fills its disk within one time to live, or rewriting a pack past half expired costs a build more than
recomputing what the pack held. Moderate confidence: both are measured on one tree's store, not on a node's.
