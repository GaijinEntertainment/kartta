# Analyze what a change reaches with the walk the call queries take

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-17
- **Revised:** 2026-10-20
- **Initiative:** `03-code-graph`

An agent about to edit a range of lines asks what the edit reaches: the symbols the lines hold, their callers and
callees, how far a change spreads through the callers of each, and the files it touches. A reviewer asks the same of a
whole change, a set of line regions in several files and languages, and asks where the reaches of its symbols meet.
Calls are resolved when a query asks (ADR 3.7), so each symbol these answers walk costs the resolution of its name's
call sites, on trees of more than a million symbols.

**A blast is the walk that `callers` takes, with a visitor in place of the list.** The reach of a change to a symbol is
the breadth-first walk over its callers that `callers` runs, under the same `Limits`: the depth bound, the symbol cap
and the confidence floor. The walk gives each symbol it reaches to a visitor. `callers` and `callees` push a `Reached`
into the list they return; the analyses count the depth and the file of each symbol and read no definition for a symbol
they do not list. One walk answers all of them, so the reach of a blast is the length of the list that `callers` returns
for the same symbol and limits.

**One query resolves each name once.** A segment or a change keeps one resolution memo for all the symbols it analyzes,
so the call sites of a name are resolved once, whichever symbol or walk asks first. A walk borrows the memoized caller
edges of each symbol it expands and does not copy them; the callees of a body are resolved for that body, as `callees`
does. Nothing outlives the query: the graph keeps nothing more for these analyses, and a build pays nothing for them.

**A declaration answers for the definition it links to.** A segment over a header lists a prototype with the callers
and callees of the definition that its callable identity links it to. A change counts a declaration and its definition
as one changed symbol, answered as the occurrence first in path order.

**Every count takes one confidence floor.** The floor of `Limits` filters the direct callers and callees, the walk, and
the caller count and consumers of a changed symbol. Each answer counts what the floor left out: `below_confidence`
counts the direct calls under it, and for a walk the edges it turned down at each symbol it expanded.

**A cut answer says how much it cut.** Direct calls list at most 50 callers and 50 callees in path order, each side with
its `total` and `below_confidence`. A blast and a changed symbol report `truncated`, the callers the cap turned away at
the symbol where it stopped the walk, and `unexpanded`: the symbols at the depth bound, the symbol itself at a depth of
0, and those the cap left in the queue. A change lists at most 300 changed symbols and 300 hotspots, and counts all of
them in `changed_symbols` and `total_hotspots`; `truncated_seeds` counts the walks the cap stopped, and
`unheld_regions` and `regions_outside_symbols` count the regions that reached no symbol. A changed symbol lists its
direct callers while 30 or fewer pass the floor, and counts them by directory past that. Changed symbols rank by reach,
then by direct callers; hotspots rank by the changed symbols that reach them, then by the nearest depth. Test,
generated and entry code ranks after all other code in both lists, because how it is run, not what it calls, sets its
reach. A change keeps at most 600 listed changed symbols at a time, each with its definition and consumers, and cuts
them back to the best 300 by that ranking; past the list it keeps only the position of each changed symbol and of each
symbol the walks reach, which the counts and the hotspots need. Revised 2026-10-20: a segment's symbol and a changed
symbol count in `ambiguous` the calls of their name that `Graph::callers` counts in `Walk::ambiguous`, which bound no
definition because several of the name may be meant, so a symbol whose every call went unbound no longer answers no
caller and no reach without saying so (ADR 4.1). The count ranks nothing.

**The files a change touches are the files of its callers and of its file's includers, each found apart.**
`affected_files` counts the files, other than the symbol's own, that hold a caller the walk reached or that include
the symbol's file within the walk's depth. The includers are found one depth at a time, as `dependents` finds them (ADR
3.19), and not through the visited set of the callers' walk, so a file that holds a caller is still followed to its own
includers.

**A reach takes a band.** A reach under 10 is `Low`, 10 to 49 is `Medium`, 50 to 199 is `High`, and 200 or more is
`Critical`; an exported symbol is `High` from 20 to 199, and an interface is `Critical` from 50, because each of its
implementors answers for its contract. These are the thresholds of the reference analysis, stated so that no reach
falls in two bands.

**The engine merges a change across language families.** A region goes to the family whose language claims its file.
The graph of each family analyzes its regions, and `Change::merge` adds the counts together and ranks the changed
symbols and hotspots of all families under one cut. The families hold different symbols, so no symbol is counted twice
in a sum. A region whose file no language claims counts as unheld. A segment is one file, which one family holds, so
the engine reaches it through `graph_of`.

## Measured

Parity is measured against the reference analysis on its own query sets, built from real symbols, on the C++
game-engine tree of 146,752 files (1,193,586 C++ symbols) and on the Go monorepo of 44,443 files (39,771 Go): per tree,
100 segments of whole symbols, whole files, partial symbols and ranges across symbols, and 60 changes of up to five
regions, a sixth of them in two languages, at the reference's defaults: a depth of 3, a cap of 500 symbols and no
confidence floor.

The comparison found one resolver defect, fixed before these numbers were taken: the include tier counted a file that
offers the name on two owners as a file that offers none, so the one definition of a second included file passed as
the unique target, and the call bound at 70 to one of three candidates.

Segments on the C++ tree: both sides find the same 2,192 symbols, includes and included files in all 100. The direct
calls differ for 113 symbols:

- 74 header declarations, which kartta answers with the calls of their definition and the reference with none;
- 25 calls through a value, such as `activeSensors.clear()` on a container, which the reference binds by the name alone
  to a method of the caller's file or its includes and kartta leaves unbound, as it does not know the value's type;
- 7 symbols of minified vendored bundles, where up to 114 definitions of one name share a line of up to 292,762
  characters, so a call's line cannot tell them apart; compared name by name, the definitions of 26 of the 388 names of
  one bundle differ, with kartta binding 142 calls against 213;
- 5 calls written qualified to a name that two definitions share, where each side binds a different one and kartta the
  first in path order;
- 1 method whose callees differ by the declaration rule and by one call the reference binds to `setView` of a class of
  the same name in another sample program;
- 1 call to a Python function that its caller defines inside itself, which kartta missed: the Python extractor marked a
  nested definition local to its body and recorded no local call to it, and a local binds only through such a call.
  Since ADR 3.12's revision of 2026-10-17 the extractor records the local call, and both sides answer that call alike.

The reach differs for 66 symbols, 29 of them through the direct calls above and 37 through calls further out. The calls
further out, in segments and in changes, show the same kinds and two more: an explicit base-class call in Python,
`OutputGenerator.genType(self, ...)`, which kartta binds to the named owner and the reference misses, and a call
through a member value whose class header the caller includes only through another header, which the include tier,
reading direct includes alone, does not reach, and the reference does. The affected files differ for 1,064 symbols,
kartta counting more for 1,041: in 1,037, the depth-first include walk of the reference cuts includers within the bound
or stops at an includer that holds a reached caller, and in 27 the callers reached differ.

Segments on the Go monorepo: both sides find the same 6,704 symbols. The includes differ for 31 files and the included
files for 82: the monorepo holds a nested checkout of itself, whose imports kartta resolves into its own `vendor` tree
and the reference into the root's (ADRs 3.2 and 3.19). The direct calls differ for 32 symbols: 26 across the two
copies of the checkout, and 6 calls through a value. The affected files differ for 6,677 symbols: 6,668 because the
direct includers differ by that import resolution, 3 because the includers further out differ by it, and 6 where the
include walk of the reference cuts includers or stops at an includer that holds a reached caller, 4 of them TypeScript
files whose one includer holds the symbol's caller.

Changes: the changed symbols and files are equal in all 60 sets on both trees. On the C++ tree, 866 of the 868 changed
symbols listed are the same on both sides, and the 2 others on each side are names of minified bundles; the reach
differs for 13 and the direct callers for 2, through the causes above and one Python call through a parameter of the
name of a module function, `chunk_fn(source, tags)`, which kartta bound to that function: the Python extractor did not
read parameters, and with nested definitions local, the function was the one candidate. Since ADR 3.12's revision of
2026-10-17 that call records nothing, and both sides answer its set alike. The hotspots differ only in the 2 sets of
vendored JavaScript bundles, 14 hit counts of the 600 both list and 1,660 and 852 hotspots in all against
1,686 and 887, and the footprint differs in those 2 sets and in 4 whose reaches differ by the causes above. On the Go
monorepo, the 303 changed symbols listed are the same and no set has a hotspot; the reach differs for 10 and the
direct callers for 8: 5 across the copies of the nested checkout, 2 further out, and 3 where kartta finds callers the
reference misses, such as `db.WarRooms().Content(id)`, through the one method of that name.

A blast walks what `callers` walks: of the segment symbols whose name selects the same symbol for `callers`, 1,263 of
1,266 on the C++ tree and 351 of 351 on the Go monorepo report the reach of the `callers` list, and of the changed
symbols, 625 of 626 and 121 of 121. Every exception is a name that several definitions share on one minified line, where
selecting by name cannot tell which of them each side walked.

## Considered Options

- **A walk of the analyses' own, as the reference has** — a second breadth-first walk to keep in step with `callers`,
  so the reach a blast reports and the callers `callers` lists could part.
- **Each walk returns its list, and the analyses count it** — a definition read and built for every symbol reached,
  where a changed symbol lists only its direct callers and a blast lists none.
- **The includers found depth first, through the callers' visited set** — the reference's count, which depends on the
  order of the walk: it counts fewer files than the includers within the bound for 1,037 symbols on the C++ tree and 6
  on the Go monorepo.
- **A memo for each symbol analyzed** — each walk would resolve again the names that the walk before it resolved, and
  the walks of one change of five regions on the C++ tree reach 2,606 symbols.
- **A segment on the engine** — a file belongs to one family, so the engine would only route the call that `graph_of`
  already routes.

Revisit when a consumer needs a whole large header analyzed faster than per-query resolution answers it, 0.3 s on the
C++ tree, or when changes of thousands of symbols make the per-query memo outgrow the few megabytes a query holds now.
