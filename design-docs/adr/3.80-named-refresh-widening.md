# Widen a named refresh to the whole tree past one part for every 30 entries

- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta/src/engine.rs` (`ENTRIES_PER_PART`, `FEWEST_PARTS`, `refresh_parts`),
  `kartta::Engine::refresh_paths`, `kartta::Engine::refresh_paths_limit`, `kartta::EngineRefresh::widened`,
  `kartta::Widened`
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-languages/tests/refresh.rs`
  (`named_paths_that_come_to_more_parts_than_the_limit_refresh_the_whole_tree`,
  `named_paths_walk_apart_up_to_one_part_for_every_30_entries`,
  `paths_past_the_limit_inside_an_ignored_directory_walk_only_that_directory`,
  `paths_past_the_limit_that_come_to_the_limit_in_parts_walk_apart`)

A followed engine refreshes the paths a watcher or a version control tool names, and the parts those paths come to cost
about their number, where a whole refresh costs about the tree's entries. Past one part for every 30 entries, and never
fewer than 50, `Engine::refresh_paths` refreshes the whole tree in their place and says so, so a caller never needs to
know where the two cross.

## Decision

- **The limit.** `Engine::refresh_paths` on an engine built with stamps walks the parts of its paths apart while they
  number at most `Engine::refresh_paths_limit`: the index's entries divided by 30, and never fewer than 50.
- **Parts, not paths.** The limit counts parts, not named paths. The parts are those `kartta_fs::Index::parts_of`
  answers ([ADR 1.16](/design-docs/adr/1.16-scoped-refresh.md)): each path widened as the index widens it, none below
  another, so a held file in a directory of its own is one part, every path inside a directory the index does not hold,
  such as an ignored `target/`, is one part together, and a path inside `.git` or through a pruned name is none. A call
  that names no more paths than the limit hands them to the index as they are; one that names more works their parts
  out first.
- **Past the limit.** A call whose paths come to more parts than the limit refreshes the whole tree, as
  `Engine::refresh` does, and `EngineRefresh::widened` gives the number of paths named, the parts they came to and the
  limit those passed. It is `None` from `Engine::refresh` and from a call that walked its parts. The MCP server renders
  it ([ADR 4.44](/design-docs/adr/4.44-mcp-refresh-tool.md)).
- **No choice for the caller.** The caller does not choose between named paths and a whole refresh.
- **The index's part.** How the index reads the named files and takes each part, on which threads, is
  [ADR 1.45](/design-docs/adr/1.45-scoped-refresh-reads.md)'s rule.

## Why

- Named paths cost about the number of their parts, and a whole refresh about the tree's entries, so the crossover
  moves with the entry count. With named files read on the index's threads, it sat near one part for every 20 entries on
  the Go monorepo snapshot of 34,506 entries and one for every 53 on the C++ game-engine tree of 182,714 (2026-10-20,
  #159); 30 lies between them, where the two modes stayed within 5% of each other past the crossover.
- A fixed count would widen a few hundred paths on a tree of millions of entries into a whole walk: 47–51 s on the
  5.5 million entries of the data volume ([ADR 1.16](/design-docs/adr/1.16-scoped-refresh.md)).
- A build inside an ignored `target/` names its outputs by the thousand. Counted as paths, they made a whole refresh of
  a change the index never holds; as parts, they are one.
- The crossover depends on the index's entries and the engine's own extraction, which the engine counts and the caller
  does not.
- At least 50 parts keeps a small tree from widening on every batch: on 2,603 entries a whole refresh took 13–19 ms and
  named paths matched it between 50 and 107 (2026-10-18, #79).

## Consequences

- A call past the limit pays `Index::parts_of` over every path it named before it walks either way, at the cost
  [ADR 1.16](/design-docs/adr/1.16-scoped-refresh.md) states; accepted.
- On a tree whose crossover lies below the limit, such as the game-engine tree, whose limit is 6,090 parts against a
  crossover near 3,429, a call between the two walks apart where a whole refresh would be as fast or faster, within 5%;
  accepted.

## Rejected

- **Widen past a fixed number of named paths** — the smaller tree's crossover, near 1,714 paths, turns a few more paths
  on a tree of millions of entries into a whole walk that costs over a hundred times what the paths do.
- **Let the caller choose between named paths and a whole refresh** — leaves every consumer to learn a crossover that
  depends on counts only the engine holds.
- **Count named paths against the limit** — a build's outputs inside an ignored directory widen into a whole refresh.

## Revisit if

The index takes the parts themselves on its threads, their stats and the ignore rules above them, which is what named
paths still pay beside a whole refresh, or a consumer follows a tree whose whole refresh costs far more for each entry
than on the measured trees, such as one on a network mount; medium confidence.

## History

- 2026-10-21 — Past the limit: an agent could not see how far past the limit a widened refresh was, or whether naming
  fewer paths would have kept it scoped, and the engine already holds the parts it counts (#241)
- 2026-10-20 — The limit: named files read on the index's threads cost what a whole refresh pays for them, which moved
  the crossover out to one part for every 30 entries from one path for every 150 (#159)
- 2026-10-20 — Parts, not paths: a build inside an ignored directory names paths that change nothing in the index, and
  counted as paths they made a whole refresh (#148)
