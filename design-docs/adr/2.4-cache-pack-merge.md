# Merge a cache's packs once they pass eight

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Updated:** 2026-10-16
- **Initiative:** `02-shared-cache`
- **Governs:** `kartta_cache::Store::merge`, `kartta_cache::Merged` (`packs`, `entries`, `published`, `left_in_place`),

  (`ranges_tile_the_index`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-cache/tests/store.rs` (`a_store_of_eight_packs_is_left_as_it_is`,

  `a_pack_whose_namespace_ranges_are_damaged_is_refused_and_never_merged_away`), `crates/kartta-cache/src/store.rs`
  (`a_pack_a_merge_removed_after_the_listing_sends_the_store_back_to_the_listing`,
  `a_pack_that_keeps_vanishing_is_given_up_after_the_last_listing`)

Every publish of [ADR 2.1](/design-docs/adr/2.1-shared-cache-store.md) adds a pack, and a lookup
searches the packs until one answers, so a store left alone answers more slowly with every build. `Store::merge` folds
the packs into fewer once they pass eight, without a lock, at the price of a merge two processes may both do.

## Decision

- Each publish adds a pack unless the same pack is already in place. A store searches its packs largest first, by
  entry count, until one answers.
- `Store::merge` leaves eight packs or fewer as they are, unless
  [ADR 2.3](/design-docs/adr/2.3-cache-entry-expiry.md) rewrites one for its expired entries. Past
  eight, while the largest pack holds more entries than all the others together, the others merge into one and the
  largest stays; otherwise every pack merges into one.
- A merge reads the packs its store mapped, so a pack published after the store opened waits for a later merge. It
  writes their entries through a writer, one entry per key and none that fails its check, publishes the merged pack,
  and only then removes the packs it merged. A pack it cannot remove stays in place, answering the same entries, and is
  counted in `Merged::left_in_place`.
- A store that mapped a merged pack keeps answering from it after it is removed. Two processes that merge the same packs
  at once each publish a merged pack and each remove only what they merged, so no entry is lost, and the next merge
  folds the duplicates.
- A store that opens while another process merges can list a pack the merge then removes. When a listed pack has gone,
  the store lists the directory again and finds the merged pack, for up to four listings. A pack still vanishing after
  the fourth, which takes a merge finishing between each two listings, is left out of that store and is not counted.
- A pack whose namespace ranges do not cover its index in ascending order is refused when it opens, so a merge never
  reads a damaged range as empty and then removes the only copy of its entries.
- The caller decides when to merge. A `kartta-fs` build merges once it has published
  ([ADR 2.2](/design-docs/adr/2.2-build-cache-lookup.md)).

## Why

- Keeping the largest pack while it outweighs the rest makes the routine merge copy only what builds added since: on
  the game-engine checkout's 149,697 entries, merging eight small packs of 2,379 entries took 0.024-0.031 s, and merging
  nine packs of one size, 227 MB, took 0.75-0.76 s (macOS, 18 cores, 2026-10-16).
- Largest first makes a hit in a store of few, uneven packs cost one search rather than one per pack.
- Publishing before removing is what lets a store that lost a listed pack find its entries by listing again.
- Only the caller knows how many processes share the directory, so only it can judge when a merge pays.

## Consequences

- Two processes that merge the same packs at once do one merge's work for nothing. Accepted.
- A store that opens while merges finish between each of its four listings misses a pack's entries for its life, and
  recomputes them. Accepted.
- A merged pack that could not be removed holds its entries twice until a later merge removes it; a build reports it
  as `BuildError::Cache`
  ([ADR 2.5](/design-docs/adr/2.5-cache-leftover-files.md)).

## Rejected

- **A lock on merging** — the store shares nothing but its files, and concurrent merges waste work without losing an
  entry.
- **Merging every pack every time** — copies the whole store on every build, 0.75 s for 227 MB against 0.03 s for what
  builds added.

## Revisit if

The merges of many processes finishing at once cost more than a lock on merging would. Low confidence either way: the
cost of concurrent merges is not measured.
