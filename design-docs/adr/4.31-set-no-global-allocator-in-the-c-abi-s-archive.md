# Set no global allocator in the C ABI's archive

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-18
- **Updated:** 2026-10-18
- **Initiative:** `04-consumer-surfaces`
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-ffi/Cargo.toml`, `crates/kartta-ffi/src/lib.rs`

The library crates stay allocator-neutral (ADR 1.18), but `kartta-ffi`'s archive of ADR 4.2 is a final artifact and
could set one. It sets none, so Kensai's static musl binaries keep musl's malloc and its lower peak, at the price of a
slower build than mimalloc's.

## Decision

The archive sets no `#[global_allocator]` and depends on no allocator crate: a consumer links the C library's malloc,
musl's in Kensai's static binaries.

## Why

- Under musl, mimalloc built about 10% faster, but peaked higher: 50-70 MB above musl tuned to purge at once and
  collect after the build, where it ended at musl's resident size, and 45% above it untuned, keeping up to half again as
  much resident (cold builds of the C++ game-engine tree, all sixteen languages, `threads` 4, an Alpine container of 4
  CPUs, 2026-10-18).
- mimalloc brings two crates and a C build into the archive.
- musl hands freed memory back without a trim: it peaked below what glibc reached with both trims in ADR 1.18, and ended
  where glibc did after them.

## Consequences

A build under musl runs about 10% slower than it would under mimalloc: accepted. The build times measured are not
comparable to ADR 1.18's, since the session's load and the mounted tree slowed both allocators alike, and no glibc
build ran beside them.

## Rejected

- **mimalloc** — about 10% faster, but a higher peak and two crates and a C build in the archive.
- **mimalloc tuned to purge at once and collect after the build** — it ends where musl does but still peaks 50-70 MB
  higher.

## Revisit if

Build time matters more to a consumer than its peak, or a glibc build of the same session shows the musl build's time
is the allocator's. Medium confidence: the musl and mimalloc times came from a loaded host and a mounted tree.
