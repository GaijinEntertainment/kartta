# Read a named refresh's files on the index's threads

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-20
- **Updated:** 2026-10-24
- **Initiative:** `01-kartta-fs-port`
- **Governs:** `crates/kartta-fs/src/index/scoped.rs` (`Rewalk::found`, `Rewalk::read_on_threads`)
- **Pinned by:** `crates/kartta-fs/tests/refresh.rs` (`the_named_files_a_refresh_reads_are_read_on_the_index_threads`,
  `named_files_read_on_threads_never_hand_out_again_an_id_a_stopped_refresh_handed_its_visitor`)

Reading and extracting files is most of what `Index::refresh_paths` (ADR 1.16) costs, and a whole refresh does it on
every thread. The calling thread takes the parts in order, and the files among them are read on the index's threads, so
a call that names many paths and does not widen to the root stays cheaper than a whole refresh, at a heap that follows
the threads while the call runs.

## Decision

- **The calling thread takes the parts.** It takes every part in tree order, with its stat and the rules above it
  (ADR 1.43), which go side by side on a slow mount
  ([ADR 1.46](./1.46-ask-a-slow-mount-about-a-named-refresh-s-parts-side-by-side.md)), and walks a directory part on
  the index's threads, as a build walks.
- **The files go to the threads.** Once every part is taken, the parts that are files are read on the index's threads,
  each file's text dropped once its visitor returns, as a build drops it.
- **One file stays.** A call with one file among its parts, or one that reads nothing because it has no visitor and no
  content index, reads on the calling thread.

## Why

- Reads and extraction were 84% and 90% of the calling thread's samples in an engine's refresh of 1,714 named paths on
  the game-engine checkout and the Go monorepo snapshot, and the splice under 0.5% (2026-10-20).
- Reading the files on the threads took 1,600 changed files from 1,919 ms to 311 ms on the Go monorepo snapshot
  (2026-10-20); what the calling thread keeps, the stats and the rules, was then 1–2% of a call's samples.
- Handing one changed file to the threads took an engine's one-file call on the game-engine checkout from a median
  1.4 ms to 1.5 ms, so a one-file call stays where it was.

## Consequences

- A call reads at once as many files as the index has threads, so it holds that many texts and parse trees: 3,429 named
  paths on the game-engine checkout raised the heap 188–203 MiB, against 135–142 MiB read in turn, given back when the
  call returns; the heap follows the threads, not the paths (accepted).

## Rejected

- **Take every part on the index's threads**, its stat, its rules and a directory's walk included — level with reading
  only the files there, 320 ms against 311 ms for 1,600 files, at a part's rules read before its stat tells whether it
  exists, and with the calling thread's share already at 1–2%.
- **Read the largest files first**, so the longest extraction starts early — slower on both trees measured: 517 ms
  against 311 ms on the snapshot.

## Revisit if

The stats and rules the calling thread keeps in turn become a large share of a call on a local disk. Medium confidence:
they were 1–2% there, and a mount slow enough to matter hands them to threads of the call's own.
